# Lunch Macro

The **11:30–1:30 ET** midday macro window inside the New York session.

> [!note] Earlier source with stricter boundary
> The 2022 mentorship Episode 5 defines the lunch no-trade window as **12:00 PM – 1:00 PM** NY local time — a stricter formulation than the later 11:30–1:30 lunch macro window. Both are valid; Episode 5 provides the earliest ICT-direct source ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-5]]). Episode 9 adds the **PM session opportunity** context: after the lunch consolidation (noon–1:00 PM), the best setups emerge at **2:00 PM onward**, especially when the morning session was invalidated by an overnight run ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-9]]).

## Core idea

In the ingested NQ sources, this is the time when the morning drive often either:
- extends toward its target,
- pauses into a retracement,
- or resumes after a sweep / rebalance into a cleaner PD array.

It is not the open. It is the mid-day pulse where the day often continues or corrects after the morning structure is already established.

## How sources use it

- The **03/22 timing + journaling** lecture names 11:30–1:30 ET as part of the time-first workflow ([[sources/2025-lecture-series-algorithmic-timing-and-journaling-03222025]] @ 58:57).
- The **NY Lunch Macro** review uses 10:50–11:10 and 11:30–1:30 as the working midday window for the move into the high ([[sources/2025-lecture-series-making-money-with-smc-concepts]] @ 0:32, @ 7:42).
- The **live tape-reading AM review** treats the lunch hour as the place where a sweep into a 15-minute FVG or order block may set up the next continuation leg in an otherwise bullish day ([[sources/nq-live-tape-reading-am-review]] @ 13:59, @ 14:11, @ 14:34).
- The **high-resistance liquidity run conditions** commentary shows the same window can still be tradable while remaining stubborn: price can sit inside an inversion FVG, demand partials, and fail to deliver cleanly ([[sources/trading-high-resistance-liquidity-run-conditions-with-my-lunch-macro]] @ 0:57, @ 1:22, @ 6:53).
- The **direct 02/24/2025 lunch-macro execution** adds a cleaner NQ example: the first hour defines the dealing range, the trade leans on an immediate rebalance / reclaimed inversion FVG, and the target is the high formed after 10:00 ([[sources/nq-ny-lunch-macro-02242025]] @ 1:18, @ 1:58, @ 4:50, @ 7:42).
- The **Understanding Time & Price** lecture ties lunch back into the whole day: PM session range, London range, AM opening range, and the midday pulse all belong to the same time-and-price routine ([[sources/understanding-time-price-the-only-thing-you-need]]).
- Lecture 12 of the 2024 mentorship adds the stop-hunt rule explicitly: when the morning has trended higher, the 11:30 lunch macro will often go backward to the **first low formed after 10:00** and raid that low before the uptrend resumes ([[sources/2024-mentorship-lecture-12]]).
- The **03/11/2026 lunch algorithmic theory** clip sharpens the carry-forward rule: take the inefficiency immediately before the lunch liquidity raid, carry it into the next day, and treat a revisit as the directional reference for the next session ([[sources/ict-2026-new-york-lunch-algorithmic-theory]] @ 10:08, @ 19:22, @ 27:12).
- The **06/10/2024 NQ lunch macro** clip adds a direct live example: a volume imbalance, IFVG, and consequent encroachment combine with the midday pulse to frame the entry after a liquidity raid ([[sources/nq-lunch-macro-06102024]] @ 2:33, @ 6:32, @ 8:44, @ 11:17).

## Practical use

Use the lunch macro as a time filter, not as a standalone signal:
- if morning structure is already complete, look for a retracement or continuation setup inside the midday pulse,
- if the morning move has not yet validated, be cautious about forcing a trade just because the clock changed,
- and keep the macro aligned with the relevant opening-range / FVG / liquidity frame.
- If the lunch raid has already occurred, prefer the pre-raid imbalance as the next-day reference over a fresh support/resistance read.
