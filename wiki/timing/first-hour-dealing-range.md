# First Hour Dealing Range

The first hour dealing range is the 9:30–10:30 New York local-time range after the cash open. It is distinct from the 30-minute opening range, which covers only 9:30–10:00 ([[sources/ict-2026-1st-hour-dealing-range-march-28-2026]], [[sources/tired-of-9-5-learn-the-9-am-crt-model-to-achieve-financial-freedom]]).

The 2022 mentorship Episode 5 provides the earliest ICT-direct morning session boundary: **8:30 AM – 11:00 AM** as the primary trading window, with the 8:30 open serving as the key inflection where news events trigger displacement ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-5]]). Episode 6 demonstrates this in live NQ action: the 8:30 AM employment data release creates the displacement that sets the morning's bearish thesis, with the 15-minute chart serving as the "bellwether" and lower timeframes offering refined FVG entries ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-6]]). Episode 7 adds the **leader trade** technique for the sloppy 9:30 open: place small single-contract entries to gather market intel before scaling in, and demonstrates the full daily bias → 9:30 liquidity sweep → MSS → FVG entry sequence in a live February 8, 2022 NQ example ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-7]]). Episode 9 adds the critical **avoid-morning-after-overnight-run** rule: if NQ has made a large move overnight (London/early morning), the 9:30–11:00 AM window is likely to be choppy and sloppy; the episode explicitly advises waiting until post-lunch (1:00 PM onward) for cleaner setups ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-9]]). Episode 10 reinforces the **15-minute chart as the "bellwether"** — ICT states that if forced to trade only one timeframe, he would choose 15-minute for its panoramic view — and introduces the **opening range** concept: on bearish days, premium arrays form between the open and the high, and subtracting this range from the open creates the optimal short entry zone ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-10]]).

The 03/28/2026 first-hour lesson is the cleaner direct source for the label: ICT uses the 9:30 open as a "change in state of delivery" marker, treats the 9:30–10:30 span as the real working scaffold, and uses the first-hour midpoint plus projected quarters / octants for continuation management ([[sources/ict-2026-1st-hour-dealing-range-march-28-2026]] @ 34:58, @ 35:17, @ 43:49, @ 50:14, @ 1:15:47, @ 1:18:04).

The 04/14/2026 futures review reinforces the same boundary from the other side: once the first-hour range is fulfilled, ICT says lunch macro can begin around the 10:30 hour and often starts by retracing back inside the morning range ([[sources/ict-2026-futures-review-april-14-2026]] @ 4:37, @ 4:50, @ 5:16, @ 27:44).

## Core idea

ICT describes the first hour as a fuller dealing frame:

- 9:30–10:00 is the opening range,
- 9:30–10:30 is the first hour dealing range,
- and the midpoint / consequent encroachment of that larger range becomes a useful reference for later continuation or reversal.

The source makes the distinction explicit so the opening-range label is not used too loosely.

This page is the canonical home for the broader 9:30–10:30 frame; the older 'first hour range' label is now treated as an alias / redirect into this page.

## Why it matters

The first hour dealing range helps separate:

- the first 30 minutes of opening action,
- from the broader first-hour structure,
- and from later lunch-macro behavior that can still reference the 10:00 or 10:30 pivots.

## Practical use

Use it to ask:

- Has the 9:30–10:30 range completed?
- Is price reacting at the midpoint of that hour?
- Is the market respecting the first-hour frame more than the shorter opening range?
