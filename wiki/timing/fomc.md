# FOMC

FOMC is the high-impact Federal Reserve event that often changes how the market behaves for the rest of the week. In the PO3 source, it is one of the major macro contexts that can make early-week price choppier because professionals wait for the event before positioning, with the first 2 p.m. rate-release move often being the false move ([[sources/simple-power-of-three]] @ 1:37, @ 2:06, @ 2:22, @ 5:57).

## Core idea

FOMC is less a setup than a context filter:

- it can suppress early-week positioning,
- it can create a false move at the first release,
- and it can determine whether the week continues into Thursday / Friday or reverses after the event ([[sources/simple-power-of-three]] @ 2:30, @ 2:51, [[sources/2025-lecture-series-nq-review-presession-trade-may-15-2025]] @ 17:15).

The 09/17/2025 FOMC lecture adds a live delivery rule: the morning can already be structured with a 9:30 shift in market structure and a first-presented FVG, but the true event-day second stage often comes after the 2:00 p.m. release / conference sequence, so traders should think in stages instead of a single impulse ([[sources/fomc-two-stage-delivery]] @ 2:19, @ 4:18, @ 17:05, @ 18:18, @ 20:22, @ 20:48).

Derivative calendar-filter teachings often use the same posture: avoid the day before FOMC, avoid the release itself, and then treat the post-release move as the part that actually matters ([[sources/how-to-use-the-economic-calendar-for-trading]]).

## Why it matters

The PO3 material treats FOMC as a major weekly inflection point inside a broader timing framework.

On FOMC weeks:

- Monday / Tuesday can be choppy,
- the market may delay the real move until after the Fed speaks,
- and unfinished higher-timeframe targets can keep the week trending after the event.
- FOMC can also print in stages: the first 2:00 p.m. release leg may be the initial delivery, while the conference / post-release leg becomes the second-stage continuation or reversal ([[sources/fomc-two-stage-delivery]] @ 17:05, @ 18:18, @ 20:22, @ 20:48).

## Practical use

Use FOMC as a risk and timing filter:

- expect reduced commitment before the release,
- treat the first 2 p.m. move as potentially false,
- and wait for the post-event direction to confirm before leaning too hard on a bias.

The 09/17/2025 lecture sharpens that into a stage-based execution rule: the morning structure can be traded if the 9:30 shift, first-presented FVG, and volume imbalance line up, but once the 2:00 p.m. event starts the trader should expect a separate second-stage leg instead of assuming the first move tells the whole story ([[sources/fomc-two-stage-delivery]] @ 2:19, @ 4:18, @ 5:48, @ 7:13, @ 17:05, @ 18:18, @ 20:22, @ 20:48).
