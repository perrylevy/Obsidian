# How to Trade When You Don't Have an Initial Bias

This page is the operator guide for the **no initial bias** state. The source's practical rule is to wait for the first hour, let the opening structure define the working range, and only then decide whether the upper half or lower half deserves attention ([[timing/no-initial-bias]], [[concepts/no-trade]], [[sources/how-do-i-engage-markets-when-i-dont-have-an-initial-bias]] @ 6:08, @ 6:41, @ 7:15, @ 12:22).

## Core idea

When the open is unclear, do not force a thesis. Let the market give you its first usable frame:
- the opening range,
- the first hour high / low,
- the midpoint / consequent encroachment,
- and any nearby gap or liquidity reference that helps define the morning draw.

The goal is not prediction. The goal is to let the first hour tell you what side is structurally important first.

If you miss the first presentation, that does not automatically mean the session is dead. Re-evaluate the structure, stay inside the same thesis if it is still valid, and look for the next clean trigger rather than chasing the original candle.

## How to read the open

### 1. Start with the first hour

If you do not yet know which side matters, submit to the first half-hour to first hour of trading instead of guessing. The source repeatedly frames the first hour as the place where the day’s working range becomes legible.

### 2. Mark the opening frame

Define the 9:30 open high and low, then split that range at 50%. The upper half is the preferred working area if the market is still bullish; the lower half becomes important if price loses the upper half and trades through equilibrium.

### 3. Use the range as the first structure to grade

The first-hour range is not just a box on the chart. It becomes the first meaningful scaffold for judging whether the market is still in bullish development, in rejection, or in transition.

### 4. Let the draw references guide you

The source points to the daily suspension block, the new week opening gap, and the opening-range consequent encroachment as useful draw / target references. Those are not separate random labels; they are part of the same morning decision framework.

### 5. Wait when the tape is one-sided

If the market is one-sided and you do not have a clean read, waiting for the first hour or for a news inflection is often the correct response. No bias means you do not need to invent one.

## Practical execution map

### A. Bullish open with no initial bias

If the market keeps holding the upper half of the opening range:
- stay focused on the upper half,
- look for confirmation through structure,
- and use the first-hour frame to judge whether continuation is likely.

### B. Breakdown through equilibrium

If price loses the upper half and breaks through 50%:
- shift attention to the lower half of the first-hour range,
- treat the original bullish read as weakened,
- and re-evaluate the draw.

### C. Morning wait state

If the open is noisy and directional commitment is missing, the correct action may simply be to wait. The source does not require a trade in the first few minutes; it requires a legible first-hour frame.

## What to avoid

- Do not force a directional thesis at the open.
- Do not ignore the first hour's structure.
- Do not confuse the opening range with a prediction.
- Do not keep the bullish read after price has clearly lost the upper half.
- Do not treat waiting as inaction; it is part of the method.

## Fast checklist

Before acting, ask:
1. Do I actually have an initial bias?
2. Has the first hour built a useful range?
3. Where is the 50% / consequent encroachment line?
4. Is the upper half still holding, or has price shifted lower?
5. Do the suspension block / NDOG / morning draw references support a trade?
