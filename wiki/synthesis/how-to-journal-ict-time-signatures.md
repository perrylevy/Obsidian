# How to Journal ICT Time Signatures

Use this operator guide when you want to turn a timing-heavy ICT lecture into repeatable study data instead of just a one-off note.

## Core rule

Record the market's timing signatures first, then annotate the PD arrays that formed inside those windows.

## What to journal

For each session, capture:

- the session open,
- the opening range,
- the opening-range gap or new-day opening gap,
- the first presented FVG,
- the macro window,
- any opening-range quadrant / standard-deviation reference,
- the PD array that actually delivered,
- any higher-time-frame roadmap or projected levels you were carrying forward,
- whether a prior-session array remained live into the current session,
- the time the move completed,
- and whether the market respected or violated the level.

## Good fields to log

- date
- instrument
- session window
- macro window
- range high / low
- gap level
- FVG level
- CE / midpoint
- target or draw on liquidity
- result
- what repeated
- what failed

## Practical workflow

1. Mark the opening structure first.
2. Mark the first valid FVG inside that structure.
3. Mark the macro that should contain the execution.
4. Write down the exact time the move began and ended.
5. Note whether the move repeated a prior pattern or failed early.
6. Keep the note short enough that you will actually reuse it.

## Why this matters

Journaling time signatures helps you see whether the edge is really time-based delivery or just a lucky setup. Over enough samples, the repeated behavior becomes obvious in a way that memory alone will not preserve.
