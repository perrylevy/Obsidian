# How to Handle CPI No-Trade Days

This page is the operator guide for CPI days where volatility creates a clean opening move but the opening thesis does not validate cleanly enough to justify forcing a trade.

## Core idea

CPI can deliver a sharp early move, but the key question is not whether the move is exciting. The question is whether the first presented FVG, opening-range frame, and liquidity draw actually validate after the CPI impulse.

If they do not, the correct trade is often no trade.

## What the source emphasizes

The CPI review shows a sell-side grab and a rally into the daily draw, but it also frames the opening work as conditional rather than automatic:

- the 10:00 NY Silver Bullet sits inside the 9:50–10:10 macro,
- the first presented FVG becomes the working draw,
- and the afternoon inversion / delivery shift shows that the morning thesis can fail to hold.

## Practical rule set

Use this CPI-day workflow:

1. Identify the CPI impulse and the session window.
2. Mark the first valid opening array or first presented FVG.
3. Check whether price validates the thesis or immediately violates it.
4. If validation is weak, stand down.
5. If the move is clean and the array holds, trade only the validated frame, not the whole noisy day.

## Why it matters

CPI days are dangerous because they can look obvious after the fact but still be messy in real time.

This playbook helps prevent:
- chasing the first impulse candle,
- overtrading after the opening thesis has already failed,
- and confusing a volatile day with a high-confidence day.
