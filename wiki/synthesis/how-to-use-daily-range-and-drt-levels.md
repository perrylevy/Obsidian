# How to Use Daily Range and DRT Levels

This page is the operator guide for turning the EP8 "Daily Range" cluster into an executable daily framework. It ties together the dealing range, DRT grading, measuring gap logic, breakaway gap logic, and the propulsion-block refinement into one working map for NQ.

## Core idea

The day can be read as a graded range rather than a flat line. In EP8, the outer quartiles and midpoint matter:
- 25 DRT and 75 DRT are the outer levels where breakaway / terminus logic tends to appear,
- 50 DRT is the equilibrium / measuring level,
- and the same grading can be nested inside an order block's internal range ([[concepts/dealing-range]], [[concepts/breakaway-gap]], [[concepts/measuring-gap]], [[sources/ep8-the-daily-range]] @ 2:42, @ 4:04).

This makes the day legible as a sequence of reactions around a structured range, not just a hunt for any visible FVG.

## How to read the range

### 1. Establish the daily dealing range

Start by defining the day’s working range and the directional program. EP8 frames the market as a buy program when plotting low-to-high and a sell program when plotting high-to-low; the level that matters depends on which leg is active ([[sources/ep8-the-daily-range]] @ 2:42).

The practical rule is:
- first define the range,
- then ask where price sits relative to equilibrium,
- then decide whether the current leg is early, mid, or late.

### 2. Use 50 DRT as the measuring reference

The most useful EP8 addition is the explicit claim that the midpoint FVG / imbalance is a measuring gap, not just another random inefficiency. When price is at 50 DRT, you are at the range’s balance point, which is where the market often reveals whether the move is only a retracement or a real continuation setup ([[concepts/measuring-gap]], [[sources/ep8-the-daily-range]] @ 4:04).

### 3. Treat 25 / 75 DRT as leg boundaries

At the outer quartiles, the market is more likely to be in breakaway or termination behavior. That is why the breakaway-gap page now separates:
- the **breakaway gap** = first gap of a new leg,
- the **measuring gap** = the midpoint / equilibrium reference ([[concepts/breakaway-gap]], [[sources/ep8-the-daily-range]] @ 2:42, @ 4:04).

### 4. Use the order block as a micro-range inside the daily range

EP8 also makes the order block more concrete: the OB’s opening price, and later the propulsion block, become refined reaction points inside the larger daily structure ([[concepts/order-block]], [[deferred/concepts/propulsion-block]], [[sources/ep8-the-daily-range]] @ 4:21, @ 4:54).

That means the daily range is not competing with OB logic. The OB sits inside the range and gives you a smaller execution frame.

## Practical execution map

### A. If price is at or near 50 DRT

Treat this as the balancing / decision zone.

Ask:
- Is the midpoint acting like a measuring gap or an actual continuation launchpad?
- Is the market respecting equilibrium or rejecting it?
- Is there a nearby OB / breaker / FVG confluence that gives the midpoint meaning?

Do not force an entry just because the price is “in the middle.” Midpoint is a reference, not a mandate.

### B. If price is near 25 DRT or 75 DRT

Treat these as leg-formation or leg-completion zones.

Look for:
- immediate rejection from a breakaway gap,
- a clean reaction at the OB opening price,
- or a valid SMT confirmation at a key level ([[concepts/smt]], [[sources/ep8-the-daily-range]] @ 12:43).

These outer quartiles are where the tape should show intent. If it does not, the range may still be incomplete.

### C. If price is using a propulsion block

The propulsion-block idea is a refinement, not a replacement for OB logic. When a later candle body becomes the continuation-sensitive zone, use it only after the original OB reaction is already established ([[deferred/concepts/propulsion-block]], [[concepts/order-block]], [[sources/ep8-the-daily-range]] @ 4:54).

In practice:
- original OB gives the first reaction,
- propulsion block gives the cleaner continuation entry,
- upper-quadrant sensitivity is the operative area.

## What this playbook helps prevent

This framework helps prevent:
- calling every gap a breakaway gap,
- confusing the midpoint with the beginning or end of the move,
- overtrading inside a range that is still balancing,
- and ignoring the difference between the first OB reaction and the later continuation refinement.

It also keeps the DRT language from becoming decorative. 25 / 50 / 75 should change what you do.

## Fast checklist

Before trading the daily range, ask:
1. Where is the market relative to 50 DRT?
2. Is price at a leg-start / leg-end zone, or at equilibrium?
3. Is the current gap a breakaway gap or a measuring gap?
4. Is there an OB or propulsion block giving a tighter reaction point?
5. Is SMT confirming the key level?

## What not to do

- Do not treat 50 DRT like a trade signal by itself.
- Do not confuse midpoint measurement with breakaway behavior.
- Do not use propulsion-block logic before the original OB has reacted.
- Do not assume every daily range needs the same entry model.
- Do not lose the range context by focusing only on a single candle.
