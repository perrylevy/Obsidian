# How to Manage Missed Entries

This page is the operator guide for the moment when the planned entry prints and you either freeze, hesitate, or simply arrive too late.

## Core idea

A missed entry is not automatically a missed trade. If the session structure is still alive, you can often re-enter using a smaller timeframe, a later macro window, or a cleaner second validation layer instead of abandoning the setup or forcing a revenge entry ([[sources/ict-2024-mentorship-how-to-manage-missed-entries-september-17-2024]] @ 4:44, @ 7:14, @ 8:22, @ 9:16).

## What to do after the miss

### 1. Check whether the session is still valid

Ask:
- Is the opening range / first-hour structure still intact?
- Is the draw still obvious?
- Has the market already delivered the move far enough that the setup is no longer fresh?

If the answer is no, the correct action may be to stand down. If the answer is yes, you can keep working the session.

### 2. Drop to the timeframe that still shows the setup clearly

ICT explicitly says you can use a 15-minute or 5-minute chart to get into a move you missed on a larger timeframe, and the lecture itself spends most of its time on 1-minute and 15-second charts ([[sources/ict-2024-mentorship-how-to-manage-missed-entries-september-17-2024]] @ 7:14, @ 8:22).

That means a miss on the initial trigger does not necessarily invalidate the idea. It may just mean your entry needs to be reframed at a different scale.

### 3. Stay aligned with the macro window

The clip keeps returning to the 9:50–10:10 style morning window and later session windows such as lunch. If you miss the first presentation, the next macro boundary can still provide a clean entry if the larger map is unchanged ([[sources/ict-2024-mentorship-how-to-manage-missed-entries-september-17-2024]] @ 9:16, @ 22:08, @ 33:44).

### 4. Do not convert the miss into emotional trading

The mistake after a missed entry is usually not the miss itself. It is the emotional reaction:
- chasing,
- overtrading,
- or taking a random replacement trade to avoid feeling left out.

The right response is to keep reading price and wait for the next valid structure.

## Drill mindset

The limit-order drill clip adds a useful companion rule: practice can be used to desensitize hesitation. Repeated executions, journaling, and smaller timeframes help remove the panic that creates missed entries in the first place ([[sources/ict-2024-mentorship-limit-orders-volatility-pinball-drills-october-01-2024]] @ 9:47, @ 17:05, @ 21:17).

## Fast checklist

Before you chase a missed setup, ask:
1. Is the session structure still valid?
2. Is there a later macro window still ahead?
3. Can I reframe the entry on a smaller timeframe without inventing a new thesis?
4. Or is this now a no-trade because the move is already gone?
