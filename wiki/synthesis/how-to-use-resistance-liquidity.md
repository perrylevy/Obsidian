# How to Use High- and Low-Resistance Liquidity

This page is the operator guide for the **high-resistance / low-resistance liquidity** framing. The practical rule is simple: target the low-resistance side and treat the high-resistance side as the post-sweep resistance or stop side of the curve ([[concepts/high-resistance-liquidity]], [[concepts/low-resistance-liquidity]], [[concepts/high-resistance-liquidity-run]], [[sources/hrlr-vs-lrlr]] @ 1:00, @ 1:21, @ 1:42).

## Core idea

- Low-resistance liquidity is the unswept, easier-to-reach side of the market.
- High-resistance liquidity is the side that has already been swept and therefore takes more structure to work through.
- Premarket on a quiet Monday can function as the lower-resistance opportunity, while the 9:30 open is often the higher-resistance side.
- The cleanest use is directional: target low resistance, place stops or invalidation on the high-resistance side.

## How to read the sequence

1. Identify the current swing and ask which side is still unswept.
2. If one side has already been raided, that side is becoming high-resistance liquidity.
3. Look for failure swings and market structure shift as the market organizes toward the cleaner target.
4. Use the low-resistance side as the preferred draw on liquidity.

## What to expect

- A sweep plus reversal often creates the high-resistance condition.
- The opposite side then becomes the next cleaner failure-swing target.
- The mean threshold can be a useful reaction point inside the broader move, but it is not a substitute for the liquidity read itself.

The 02/23/2026 SMC lecture gives the practical Monday test: if price is still compressing inside the same prior-candle range and the discount wick cannot close below CE, treat the low as deferred and wait for the market to earn the continuation instead of forcing the trade ([[sources/ict-2026-smart-money-concepts-lecture-february-23-2026]] @ 4:54, @ 9:04, @ 13:00, @ 13:32).

## Best use cases

Use this framing when:
- the chart is already showing one side has been raided,
- you want to know which side is cleaner to target next,
- or you are deciding where to place stop loss versus objective.

## What to avoid

- Don’t treat every swing as equally easy.
- Don’t ignore whether the sweep already happened.
- Don’t confuse the target side with the resistance side.
- Don’t skip structure confirmation when the move is no longer clean.
