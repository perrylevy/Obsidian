# How To Actually Trade Inversion Fair Value Gaps (IFVG)

## Summary

- Defines FVG as a three-candle pattern where candle one's wick and candle three's wick do not overlap, leaving a gap (@ 0:18).
- Defines IFVG as what happens when an FVG is "not respected and swiftly closed over" — former resistance becomes support (bullish) and former support becomes resistance (bearish) (@ 0:38).
- Main mistake: pattern-trading IFVGs without higher-timeframe alignment. Must anchor to swing points and HTF context (@ 1:07).
- Valid IFVG setup within the fractal model requires: a candle two that sweeps candle one's high/low, a small wick supporting expansion, then the inversion. SMT between candle one and candle two highs is extra confluence (@ 2:03).
- When the opposing candle has a large wick/large opposing run, expansion typically waits for the next HTF candle to open — wait for that open before entering (@ 6:26, @ 7:22).
- Series of stacked FVGs should be treated as one aggregated FVG for inversion purposes (@ 5:08, @ 10:02).
- Top-down example uses daily bias, 4H candle-two sweep of PDH, then 15m fractal model with inversions for entries (@ 8:14).
- An inversion can stand in place of a "change in the state of delivery" when looking for continuation entries (@ 7:22).

- **@ 2:15** — "If you want extra confluence, then look for an SMT between candle one's high and candle two's high."
- **@ 4:00** — "We have an inversion and a change in the state of delivery, which means it is valid to be looking for a move lower here."
- **@ 5:08** — "There are multiple fair value gaps here. So, in this case, I want to use all of those fair value gaps and treat it like one big fair value gap."

- **@ 8:27** — "After 3 days of expansion, I don't want to trade in the same direction. So, I will generally be looking for a reversal… Off of previous day high."
