## Summary

- ICT keeps the frame on the E-mini S&P 500 futures contract and reads the day visually through equilibrium, premium, and discount rather than indicators.
- The fib is just to highlight exactly where equilibrium is: anything above that level is premium and anything below it is discount.
- The market first drops into discount, then rotates higher into premium to work the morning high and other buy-side objectives.
- A fair value gap is the working array on the retracement side, and price can revisit it after the initial run.
- Relative equal lows and sell-side liquidity are taken out on the way higher, then the market consolidates and clears sell-side liquidity again on the later leg.
- The practical lesson is that no indicators are needed when the delivery structure and liquidity map are clear.

## Key sections

### Equilibrium and bias
- Draw the midpoint across the active range.
- Read premium versus discount visually.
- Let the swing structure, not indicators, tell you where price is relative to value.

### Liquidity target selection
- Watch the morning high as an obvious buyside objective.
- Expect price to work through sell-side liquidity before the final expansion.
- Keep the destination in view before committing to the trade.

### FVG and delivery
- Use the fair value gap as the active entry/return zone.
- Treat repeated retracements as part of the delivery sequence rather than a failure.
- Wait for the structure to resolve before expecting the expansion leg.

### Trade discipline
- Keep stops under the relevant swing low.
- Use partial exits and manage heat instead of forcing a single all-or-nothing outcome.
- If the weekly objective is done, sit on hands rather than overtrade.
