# 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 7 - Daily Bias & Consolidation Hurdles

**Video**: [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G8-z91acgG4) | **Duration**: 1:00:16 | **Channel**: The Inner Circle Trader | **Published**: February 8, 2022

## Summary

This episode introduces **daily bias** — the practice of analyzing the daily chart to determine whether price is likely to draw to an old high (bullish bias) or an old low (bearish bias). It covers how to maintain that bias until the target is reached, how to handle **consolidation hurdles** when bias becomes unclear, and the critical **intermarket relationship** between the Dow, NASDAQ, and S&P 500 (the "three averages") to confirm accumulation/distribution through divergence. The episode includes a live NQ trading example on February 8, 2022, demonstrating the full process from daily bias → intraday execution → FVG entry.

### Core themes

1. **Daily bias** — determine on the daily chart: will price draw to old high or old low? Maintain bias until target is reached.
2. **Consolidation hurdles** — when price consolidates around equilibrium, bias weakens; shift to intraday charts and hunt liquidity pools with "surgical strikes."
3. **Intermarket correlation (three averages)** — monitor Dow, NASDAQ, and S&P 500 simultaneously; when one refuses to confirm the others' move, it signals accumulation/distribution and confirms bias.
4. **Risk management based on bias** — with bias = 3.5–4.5% risk per trade (max leverage); against bias = 0.5–1% risk (significantly reduced).
5. **Leader trades** — small single-contract entries to gather market intel before scaling in.
6. **Embrace imperfection** — no perfect entry, no perfect exit, no magic bullet; small losses are premium payments for information.

## Key sections

### Daily bias

**Definition**: Daily bias is determined by analyzing the daily chart to identify the likely direction price will move next. It answers: *"Is price likely to draw higher to an old high, or lower to an old low?"*

**Key principle**: Maintain bias until price reaches the target level (old high or old low).

**How to establish daily bias**:
1. **Identify the range** — find the high and low of the current price action.
2. **Look for market structure shifts** — determine if price has broken above or below key levels.
3. **Count directional candles** — observe the number of down-close candles (bearish bias) or up-close candles (bullish bias).
4. **Expect retracement** — once liquidity is taken out, price typically retraces back into the range.

**Example from video**: NASDAQ daily chart showed price took out sell-side liquidity below a swing low → expected retracement back into the range (high to low) → bearish bias established until price reaches the old low.

### Consolidation hurdles

When price consolidates around **equilibrium** (midpoint between high and low):
- Bias becomes unclear, even for experienced traders.
- Traditional daily bias signals weaken.
- **Solution**: Shift to intraday charts and hunt liquidity pools.

**Strategy during consolidation**:
- Trade intraday volatility.
- Target old highs and old lows.
- Use **"surgical strike"** approach: take your points and exit.
- Don't overstay your welcome in the market.

### Intermarket relationships (the three averages)

**Key insight**: Monitor Dow, NASDAQ, and S&P 500 simultaneously to identify divergences that confirm accumulation/distribution.

> "These averages tend to move in tandem so that means they generally move in the same direction."

**Example from live trading**:
- NASDAQ and S&P traded below old lows.
- Dow refused to make a lower low.
- This divergence = unwillingness to deliver lower.
- Confirmed bullish accumulation setup.

**Important distinction**: This divergence doesn't *cause* the move — it *confirms* an idea you already established based on price structure.

### Risk management & leverage

**With bias (aligned with daily direction)**:
- Use maximum leverage: 3.5–4.5% risk per trade.
- Example: if max leverage = 10 contracts, use full allocation.

**Against bias (counter-trend)**:
- Dial back leverage significantly.
- Use 0.5–1% risk per trade.
- Reduces exposure to adverse moves.

**Recommended starting point**:
> "You should be risking less than 1%, about a half percent or a quarter percent because if you don't know what you're doing there's no reason to be putting high levels of risk behind a move."

**Why**: Prevents toxic thinking, bad habits, and fear-based decision making.

### Fair value gaps & market structure

**Identifying FVGs**:
- **Displacement Low**: First candle down.
- **Displacement High**: Next candle up.
- **Gap**: The imbalance between them.

**Using FVGs for entry**:
1. Price trades below old low (takes out sell-side liquidity).
2. Market structure shifts bullish.
3. Look for fair value gap to trade into.
4. Enter long when price reaches the FVG.

**Stop loss**: Place just below the low of the candle that formed the FVG.

### Live trading example: February 8, 2022

**Setup**:
- **Daily bias**: Bearish (expected lower prices).
- **Time**: 8:30 AM crosshairs (start looking left for liquidity).
- **First low identified**: Used as reference point.
- **Old low**: Target for sell-side liquidity sweep.

**Execution timeline**:

**9:30 AM opening**:
- Sloppy opening (characteristic of 9:30 opens).
- Placed **"leader trades"** (single contracts) to get market feel.
- Purpose: gather intel, not profit.

**10:00 AM**:
- Price took out sell-side liquidity.
- Market structure shifted bullish.
- FVG identified for entry.

**Entry**:
- Entered long at FVG.
- Stop below FVG low.
- Target: old high / equilibrium.

**Result**:
- Trade worked; price delivered to target.
- Demonstrated the full daily bias → intraday execution → FVG entry sequence.
