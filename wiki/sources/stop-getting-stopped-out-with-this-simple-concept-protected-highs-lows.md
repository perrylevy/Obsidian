# Stop Getting Stopped Out With This Simple Concept - Protected Highs & Lows

## Summary

- The lesson is about stop placement, not about squeezing the tightest possible stop or chasing the highest RR print.
- A protected high or low is a swing point with SMT attached; the strongest examples are time-based highs and lows.
- The practical rule is simple: if you want to avoid getting wicked out, do not place your stop at the obvious high/low or inside an obvious PD array just to save points.
- The clip repeatedly ties protection to higher-time-frame context, lower-time-frame entry, and the idea that the stop belongs beyond the swing that has already been validated by divergence.
- The source is a derivative-teacher execution/risk-management bridge: it strengthens the SMT, time-alignment, and smart-money-reversal cluster rather than creating a new ICT-direct doctrine family.
