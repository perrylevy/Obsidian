## Summary

Episode 22 centers SMT as an intermarket divergence tool: compare correlated instruments, watch which leg refuses to confirm a sweep, and use that crack in correlation as a confirmation clue rather than an entry signal by itself. The clip uses a bearish daily setup on ES, relative equal lows as the liquidity draw, imbalance / FVG behavior around the open, and later a 5-minute / afternoon divergence sequence to reinforce the same idea.

## Key sections

### 1. Daily chart context
- Bearish order block on the daily chart becomes the reference area.
- Seasonal tendency and dollar-strength backdrop support the lower-price thesis.
- Relative equal lows act like a magnet for price.

### 2. Imbalance and gap recognition
- Price action between candle low and opening price is treated as an imbalance.
- Candle bodies hold the narrative; wicks are the liquidity probes.
- The imbalance / gap becomes the opportunity zone.

### 3. Premium / discount framing
- Trading above an old high is short-term premium.
- Trading below an old low is short-term discount.
- Overbought / oversold does not prevent continuation.

### 4. Power of Three delivery scaffold
- The clip frames the session as open / manipulation / distribution.
- Smart money sells into the stop run and covers below the target lows.

### 5. Intraday execution examples
- 8:30 open and 9:30 bell both matter in the breakdown.
- Relative equal highs, displacement, and FVGs appear in the morning sequence.
- Afternoon retracement / divergence repeats the same logic.

### 6. SMT divergence
- Compare ES against NQ.
- NQ prints the stronger overnight high while ES fails to match it.
- That non-confirmation is read as weakness in ES and a sign of distribution.
