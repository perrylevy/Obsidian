# Introduction To SMT Divergence

## Summary

- **SMT stands for Smart Money Tool** (not "technique" or "trap"). It refers to divergence among correlated assets — brief moments when two or more closely correlated instruments fall out of sync — and is framed here as the single most important ICT confluence (@ 0:00–0:34).
- **SMT without time is irrelevant.** Time-anchored highs and lows are mandatory; a divergence on a random bar is dismissed as engineered liquidity, not a reversal signal (@ 0:34).
- **Bearish SMT** is identified at the highs: one asset makes a higher high (takes out buy-side liquidity) while the correlated asset makes a lower high, failing to reach the same level. The asset that made the lower high is classified as the weaker pair and preferred for shorting (@ 3:19–4:49).
- **Bullish SMT** is the mirror: one asset makes a lower low (sweeps sell-side liquidity) while the correlated asset holds a higher low. The asset that refused to go lower is classified as the stronger pair and preferred for longs (@ 5:10–6:02).
- **Three asset groups are discussed**: (1) US equity index futures — ES, NQ, YM (S&P 500, NASDAQ, Dow Jones); (2) FX currency triad — DXY, EUR/USD, GBP/USD; (3) Crypto duo — Bitcoin vs Ethereum (mentioned but not traded/charted by the presenter) (@ 4:05–7:55).
- **Multi-stage (stacked) SMT** increases probability: higher time frame SMT confirmed by middle time frame SMT confirmed by lower time frame SMT. Example HTF anchor: previous week high/low; example MTF anchor: previous 4-hour high/low (@ 8:35–11:30).
- **SMT is confirmation only** — it confirms the end of manipulation. It does not stand alone as an entry signal. Market structure must align; SMT confirms the crack in correlation, not the other way around (@ 7:55–14:21).

## Instrument-specific detail

### ES / NQ / YM (Index futures)

- Preferred trio: S&P 500 (ES), NASDAQ (NQ), Dow Jones (YM) (@ 4:05).
- Bearish example: ES makes higher high, YM makes higher high, **NQ makes lower high** → NQ is the weaker pair → enter short on NQ (@ 4:28–4:49).
- In that example NQ dropped 2.16% vs ES −1.57% and YM −1.8%, consistent with NQ being the one with the failure swing (@ 4:49–5:10).
- Note from presenter: regardless of which index makes the lower high, NQ tends to be the one that moves the most (@ 4:05).

### DXY / EUR/USD / GBP/USD (Currency triad)

- DXY is inverse to EUR/USD and GBP/USD (@ 6:17).
- Bullish example on currencies: GBP/USD fails to make a lower low, EUR/USD fails to make a lower low, DXY makes a higher high → Euro and Pound are the stronger pairs → enter long on EUR/USD and GBP/USD instead of shorting DXY (@ 6:39–7:22).

### Bitcoin / Ethereum (Crypto duo)

- Mentioned as a correlated pair but presenter does not trade crypto; no chart examples given (@ 7:22–7:55).
