## Summary

- ICT frames the day from a bullish daily bias: the market has already delivered toward 4,000, and the next consideration is the 4,095–4,100 area after an intraday push toward roughly 4,074.
- Order blocks are read by their sensitive areas: the low, the open, and the middle of the candle range, with the mean threshold at 50% acting as the continuation line.
- Fibonacci is applied across the candle so price can be judged in discount versus equilibrium before continuation.
- Fair value gaps are the active intraday array, and repeated retracements into the working FVG are part of the compression phase before expansion.
- The 5-minute and 15-minute examples emphasize two-stage delivery: an initial run, a retracement, then a stronger continuation leg.
- SMT divergence compares ES and NQ: Nasdaq can make the lower low while ES refuses to confirm, which points to bullish accumulation rather than simple weakness.
- The divergence only matters if you know the bias and what price is reaching for; otherwise the signal can vanish as correlated markets realign.

## Key sections

### Order block reading
- Watch the candle low, open, and middle of the range.
- The main threshold at 50% is the key continuation line.
- Use the OB as a specific price sensitivity area, not a vague zone.

### SMT divergence
- Compare correlated index futures, especially ES and NQ.
- A non-confirming leg is the tell.
- Divergence without bias is fragile.

### Intraday delivery
- FVGs become the working retracement array.
- Price can revisit the same discount pocket multiple times before expansion.
- Compression precedes the larger move higher.

### Trading lesson
- Know the destination before reading divergence.
- Do not short against bullish bias.
- Let structure and liquidity do the work.
