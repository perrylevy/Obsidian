# How to trade Turtle Soups (Liquidity Sweeps)

## Summary

- Turtle Soup is a liquidity sweep — its origin is the Turtle Trader breakout strategy where trend traders buy-stop above swing highs with stops below swing lows, and the market reverses right after taking that high (@ 0:28, @ 2:11, @ 2:35).
- Liquidity = swing points. A swing high and a swing low are liquidity because that's where breakout buy-stops and protective stop-losses cluster (@ 3:00, @ 3:17).
- Distinction: a **liquidity sweep** trades above/below a swing point and *immediately reverses*; a **liquidity run** trades through and continues in the same direction (@ 3:48, @ 4:15, @ 4:34).
- The mechanism is large-entity order matching — big players need opposing liquidity (resting stops) to fill size, so they sweep highs/lows to harvest the stops as their fills and then target the opposite side where stops become their take-profit (@ 4:51, @ 6:08, @ 8:05).
- Confirmation of a sweep vs a run = "comfortable above a high / below a low." A sweep is aggressive and immediate (FVG / expansion candle back the other way); a run lingers above the level (@ 8:30, @ 9:34, @ 9:54).
- Two lenses on the same sweep: **order-flow sweep** (swing high/low on current timeframe) and **candle-science sweep** (PCH/PCL on higher timeframe = swing points on lower timeframe) — fractal (@ 10:26, @ 10:50, @ 18:53, @ 21:16).
- "Last line of defense" focus: sweep setups work best when the leg has **no FVG** (no order-flow lack). If the leg contains an FVG, expect a run, not a sweep (@ 11:47, @ 12:11, @ 12:29).
- Once a FVG / fair-value area has been traded into, it's mitigated — delete it. Subsequent reactions are off the **swing point left behind** at that FVG, not the FVG itself (@ 16:37, @ 17:02, @ 17:52).
