## Summary

- The review frames the 9:30 open as a Judas swing lower into bullish support factors: a daily bullish order block, a new-week opening gap, and a 5-minute inversion FVG that should not be shorted against the higher-time-frame buy program ([[sources/nq-live-tape-reading-am-review]] @ 6:27, @ 7:28, @ 8:35, @ 9:16).
- In the bullish morning sequence, the clean long is the one where price closes above the inversion FVG and the next candle trades back down into it; that is the buy, not the initial 9:30 dump ([[sources/nq-live-tape-reading-am-review]] @ 10:38, @ 11:09, @ 12:20).
- The clip also shows a more aggressive derivative-tape variant: when price is above midnight and 8:30 opens and buy side has already been run, a bullish IFVG can be sold inside immediately as it begins to fail, without waiting for a full return to the gap ([[sources/nq-live-tape-reading-am-review]] @ 26:32, @ 26:56, @ 27:28).
- For the lunch window, the trader expects a still-bullish day to sweep sell side into a 15-minute FVG or order block before continuation, rather than just grinding higher into lunch ([[sources/nq-live-tape-reading-am-review]] @ 13:59, @ 14:11, @ 14:34).
- The session repeatedly stresses not to chase the initial lower move; if the higher-time-frame support factors are intact, the market is likely building a long off the support stack.
- This is a useful derivative-teacher bridge source because it ties IFVG execution, midnight / 8:30 anchors, NWOG support, and lunch-macro behavior into one tape-reading review.

## Key takeaways

- 6:27 — the 9:30 drop is a Judas swing, not a short to chase.
- 7:28 — bullish order block + taken sell side + NWOG support make the morning bias higher.
- 9:16 — close above the 5-minute inversion FVG, then the next candle pullback = long.
- 11:09 — the 9:46 entry sits ahead of the 9:50–10:10 macro.
- 13:59 — lunch can set up via a sweep into a 15-minute FVG.
- 26:32 — above midnight and 8:30 opens, the same IFVG can fail as a short opportunity.
- 27:28 — aggressive IFVG shorting inside the gap is used when the higher-time-frame draw is clear.

## Notes

- The clip is a strong bridge example: it does not introduce a new doctrine family, but it sharpens how a live trader selects between IFVG continuation, IFVG failure, and lunch continuation behavior.
- The morning and lunch halves both point back to the same rule: do not fade the support stack too early.
- The aggressive failure-short variant should be treated as supplementary to the more conservative HTF-aligned IFVG playbook.
