# 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 4 - Practical Trading Examples

**Video**: [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L-ReMHiavPM) | **Duration**: 8:43 | **Channel**: The Inner Circle Trader | **Published**: January 27, 2022

## Summary

This episode is a compact practical demonstration using real market data from Wednesday (Jan 26) and Thursday (Jan 27) 2022. ICT demonstrates how to identify and execute trades based on market structure shifts, fair value gaps, and equilibrium price points using Fibonacci retracements. The episode reinforces the "deer track" analogy: you must know what the setup looks like to stalk it effectively.

### Core themes

1. **Setup specificity** — setups either exist or they don't; you cannot force them.
2. **Market structure shift + FVG** — the required sequence for valid entry.
3. **Equilibrium (50%) as target** — Fibonacci retracement midpoint as a draw level.
4. **Pattern recognition training** — use weekend chart study to build visual fluency.
5. **Documentation discipline** — track time metrics, drawdown, and targets in a study journal.

## Key sections

### The Deer Track Analogy

> "you have to know what a deer track looks like you have to know what a deer track is to stalk it effectively well this is your pattern and setup so you need to go in looking for it"

Pattern recognition is the core skill. The trader must train their eye to see the recurring signatures (MSS + FVG + equilibrium target) across different market instances.

### Core setup requirements

Before entering a trade, all of the following must be present:

1. **Market structure shift** (or break) — price breaks a key swing low/high.
2. **Fair value gap** — an imbalance left by the displacement.
3. **Price breaks below/above key levels** — confirming the directional thesis.
4. **Setup cannot be forced** — "it's there or it's not."

### Example 1: E-mini S&P 500 March Futures (Wednesday, Jan 26)

**Chart**: 2-minute.
**Process**:
- Identify the day's low-to-high range.
- Apply Fibonacci retracement; the 50% level marks equilibrium.
- Price runs into premium (expensive), then breaks down below old high.
- No FVG initially — no trade.
- Price rallies again, then breaks down with a market structure break.
- FVG forms — this is the ideal entry point.
- Target: old low below the equilibrium price point.

**Hypothetical outcome**: Short entry ~4419, cover ~4382 (~37 points).

### Example 2: E-mini NASDAQ Futures (Thursday, Jan 27)

**Chart**: 5-minute (start high, work down to lower timeframes).
**Process**:
- Identify low-to-high with relative equal highs.
- Initial objective: break through relative equal highs.
- First breakout did NOT create a fair value gap → **no trade setup**.
- Liquidity rested above old high; price went higher (no intraday MSS yet).
- Swing low identified on a specific candle.
- Candle traded through swing low → **opportunity triggered**.
- FVG confirmed between high and candle low → **short entry at FVG**.
- Targets: equilibrium price point, imbalance zones.
- Result: hit all targets and went lower.

### Step-by-step methodology

1. Identify the range (low to high).
2. Apply Fibonacci to find equilibrium (50% level).
3. Wait for market structure shift (break of swing low/high).
4. Confirm fair value gap exists.
5. Enter at fair value gap (ideal entry point).
6. Target equilibrium or imbalance zones.

### Study journal requirements

Track in your study journal:
- Entry and exit prices.
- Time from market structure shift to FVG entry.
- Time from entry to target.
- Drawdown/heat experienced during trade.
- Whether all targets were hit.
