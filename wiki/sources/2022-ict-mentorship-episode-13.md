# 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 13 - Precision Price Action Execution & Risk Management

**Video**: [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tpPtItWqmlg) | **Duration**: 46:52 | **Channel**: The Inner Circle Trader | **Published**: February 25, 2022

## Summary

ICT's **practical execution lesson**, applying the previous night's advanced market structure teaching to live demo tape. A $10,000 demo account runs to $12,111 (21.11% gain) on a single pyramided trade in micro NQ futures, demonstrating that disciplined position sizing and order-block entry logic produce repeatable results. The episode weaves risk management philosophy, emotional-readiness criteria, and a formalised three-part order-block requirement into one execution frame.

### Core themes

1. **Three-part order-block requirement** — the gap/imbalance + down/up-closed candle + underlying liquidity narrative must all be present for high-probability OBs.
2. **Pyramid position sizing** — stable foundation structure (3-2-1 from largest to smallest base), never inverted.
3. **Stop-loss placement** — below the low of the OB candle, never inside the OB itself; raised as new OBs form.
4. **Demo trading prerequisites** — 6-month minimum consistent demo performance, documented plan, and emotional desensitisation required before live trading.
5. **Monthly 20% percentage-based goal** — a target to aim for, not a guarantee; compounding effects are the point.
6. **1992 cautionary tale** — ICT's own failure from rushing into live trading without preparation.
7. **Live execution example** — multi-timeframe OB identification (hourly → 5m → 1m → 30s) with pyramided entry and managed stops.

## Key sections

### The three-part order block requirement

ICT formalises the order block entry filter into three mandatory components:

1. **Gap / Imbalance** — an unfilled price area (FVG) within the candle series.
2. **Directional closed candle** — a down-closed candle for a bullish OB; an up-closed candle for a bearish OB.
3. **Underlying liquidity narrative** — price is likely to reach for buyside liquidity (bullish) or sellside liquidity (bearish).

> "It must have the imbalance coupled with the down closed candle and the underlying narrative that it's likely to go higher to reach for buy side liquidity — period."

If any of these three is missing, the order block is lower-probability.

### Pyramid position sizing

ICT prescribes a **stable foundation** pyramid structure for multiple-entry trades:

- **First entry**: largest position (3 contracts in the demo example)
- **Second entry**: medium position (2 contracts)
- **Third entry**: smallest position (1 contract)

The inverted pyramid (1-2-3, building larger as price moves further) is explicitly warned against — it is unstable and creates oversized exposure at the worst average price. The stable pyramid places the most capital at the earliest, best-priced entry.

### Stop-loss placement strategy

- Stops placed **below the low of the order block candle**, not inside the OB.
- Stops inside the OB create premature exits — price often probes the OB before continuation.
- Once price rallies and forms new down-closed candles (bullish context), stops can be **raised** below the new candle low.
- This trailing-stop logic uses the OB framework for both initial protection and ongoing management.

> "If I put my stop loss inside this area here I'm stopped out prematurely. Chances are you probably wouldn't have the wherewithal to get back in."

### Demo trading prerequisites

ICT enumerates concrete readiness criteria before transitioning to live capital:

1. **Solid price action model** — defined and tested.
2. **Documented trading plan** — entry, stop, risk amount, targets, partials.
3. **6 months minimum** of consistent demo account performance.
4. **Emotional desensitisation** — indifference to individual losses.
5. **Complete understanding** of when to engage and when to disengage.

> "If you feel this tug of war, this butterfly feeling in your stomach where you just can't relax and you're engaging in the marketplace — you're not ready."

### Monthly 20% goal

ICT uses a **percentage-based goal** rather than a dollar target:

- **Monthly**: 20% return (aspirational, not guaranteed)
- **Weekly**: specific targets within the month
- **Daily**: specific targets within the week

> "20 isn't money — it's a percentage. I don't care if you start with one dollar. If you make 20 consistently, the compounding effects of that is astonishing."

This structure provides a consistent measuring stick regardless of account size and reinforces the process-over-dollar mindset.

### The 1992 failure

ICT's personal cautionary example:
- Rushed into live trading with one book and zero backtesting
- Lost entire first trade (50% drawdown)
- Core lesson: **never determine trade management once already in the trade** — plan everything before entry

### Live execution example breakdown

1. **Hourly chart**: identified bullish OB with imbalance
2. **5-minute chart**: confirmed OB structure
3. **1-minute chart**: entry execution points
4. **30-second chart**: final entry precision

The trade entered a down-closed candle OB with an internal FVG, targeting buyside liquidity above relative equal highs. Three pyramided entries (3-2-1 contracts) with stops below the OB low. Once price rallied and formed new down-closed candles, stops were raised. Final outcome: $2,111 gain from $10,000 starting capital (21.11%).
