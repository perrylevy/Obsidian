## Summary

Episode 18 is a process lesson: start on the daily chart, establish bias, identify prior-day highs and lows as liquidity pools, then transpose the framework down to the hourly, 15-minute, and 5-minute charts. The episode also tightens ICT's order block theory by reinforcing that an OB must contain imbalance, consist of consecutive candles, and function as a change in state of delivery rather than a generic supply/demand zone.

## Key doctrine notes

### Daily bias and liquidity

The episode explicitly treats the daily chart as the source of the directional thesis. The practical sequence is: mark FVGs, mark the previous day's high/low references, establish bias, then work down into lower timeframes only after the daily roadmap is clear.

### Order block theory

The episode re-centers order blocks around three ideas:

- the block must contain an imbalance,
- the structure should be made of consecutive candles,
- and the OB is a delivery-change zone, not a generic zone of convenience.

The summary also warns against assuming the last candle is automatically the tradeable level when a higher-timeframe parent imbalance dominates.

### New York session workflow

The 7:00 AM to 10:00 AM New York window is the active killzone in the episode summary. The model expects displacement during that window, and if the trade does not fill by 11:30 AM, the order is cancelled rather than left working indefinitely.
