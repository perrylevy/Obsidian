# How To Read Price With Or Without A Bias - April 29, 2024

## Summary

- ICT frames bias as a probability filter, not as a requirement to know the exact daily high or low in advance; the important question is where the largest probability of movement sits (@ 5:08, @ 16:55).
- He starts the bias-building process from seasonal tendencies and broader monthly / weekly context, then narrows to the day and session instead of trying to force a one-minute opinion (@ 9:04, @ 15:29, @ 20:40).
- On event-heavy weeks, especially around red- and orange-folder releases such as FOMC and NFP, he explicitly advises against forcing a daily bias and treats the calendar as a manipulation map rather than a prediction engine (@ 26:52, @ 27:52, @ 1:01:19–1:02:02).
- The pre-news playbook is classic Judas swing logic: push price into a short-term low or liquidity pool, trap breakout traders, and then reverse into the intended move once the event has done its work (@ 31:39–32:24, @ 38:37–39:24, @ 43:04–45:14).
- He keeps coming back to PO3 / AMD as the framework underneath the read: accumulation, manipulation, and distribution repeat on every timeframe, including the annual seasonal map (@ 20:40–21:11, @ 47:18).
- The session takeaway is that you can trade without a bias when the tape is not aligned, but once seasonal, macro, and structure all agree, the bias becomes much more durable (@ 50:52, @ 51:15, @ 59:54).
