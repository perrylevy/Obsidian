# High Resistance vs Low Resistance Liquidity — TTrades

Derivative teacher (TTrades). Covers the canonical contrast between [[concepts/high-resistance-liquidity-run]] and [[concepts/low-resistance-liquidity-run]], using a PDF diagram and seven live chart examples on NQ/MNQ and ES.

---

## Summary

- **Failure swing** is the foundational concept: a swing high followed by a *lower* high before reversal (or a swing low followed by a *higher* low before reversal) — the original extreme was **never swept**. @ 0:05–0:37
- **Low-resistance liquidity (LRL)** = a failure swing. Liquidity rests at an unswept high or low. Because no prior sweep occurred, price has an unobstructed path to that level. @ 0:37–1:00
- **High-resistance liquidity (HRL)** = the *opposite*: price first **sweeps** through a prior extreme, wiping the resting liquidity there, before reversing. The new extreme (beyond the sweep) becomes high-resistance because the crowd's stops have already been taken. @ 1:00–1:42
- **Directional rule**: target LRL as your draw on liquidity; place stops *behind* HRL (or use HRL as the condition that confirms the move has room). @ 1:42–2:13
- **Chart identification workflow (daily bias)**: mark where failure swings stack up (LRL) and where a recent sweep created HRL. If HRL is on one side and failure swings on the other, bias toward the failure-swing side. @ 4:13–5:15
- **Entry confirmation**: after HRL is formed (sweep occurs), look for a [[concepts/market-structure-shift]] or [[concepts/change-in-state-of-delivery]], then seek entry on a [[concepts/fair-value-gap]] or [[concepts/order-block]]. @ 1:42–2:13
- **Stop placement**: stop sits on or just beyond the HRL extreme; if price returns and *closes* back over the swept level it validates a new order block and the invalidation shifts. @ 10:22–10:58

### Definitions

> "What a failure swing is is when we have a swing high and then we make a lower high relative to that before reversing — this high never got swept before the reversal."

> "Low resistance liquidity is just a failure swing … liquidity resting above highs and lows [that were never swept]."
> @ 0:37

> "High resistance liquidity is the opposite of low resistance liquidity — it is where we first sweep that liquidity before the reversal. You can see here we have a high, we make a higher high before reversing."
> @ 1:00

> "We have a low, we make a lower low before reversing, and then what this does is wipes out the liquidity resting right here and right here. So now the liquidity is resting above this high and low — and that is what creates high resistance liquidity."

> "Simply put — I want to be targeting low resistance liquidity and having high resistance liquidity for my stop loss or on my side of the curve."

## Chart signatures — quick reference

| State | Price action signature | Resting liquidity location | Trader action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Low-resistance liquidity (LRL)** | Failure swing: lower high after high, or higher low after low — original level unswept | At the unswept extreme | **Target** — bias price toward it as [[concepts/draw-on-liquidity]] |
| **High-resistance liquidity (HRL)** | Sweep: new high above old high (or new low below old low) before reversal | Now *beyond* the swept extreme | **Stop behind** — or use as confirmation of valid directional leg |

@ 0:37–1:42
