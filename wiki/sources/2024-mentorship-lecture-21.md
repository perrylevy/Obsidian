# ICT 2024 Mentorship — Lecture #21 (August 30, 2024)

Lecture 21 is a holiday-weekend execution lecture. ICT keeps the chart brutally simple: use the layered NDOG / NWOG / opening-range stack as an x-ray view of the day, treat the first valid 1-minute FVG after the 9:30 open as the working array, and refuse to force short-term trades when the session is muddy, choppy, or too light to justify aggression. The live example also sharpens immediate rebalance and change-in-state-of-delivery language by showing how a candle's opening price can become the precise re-pricing marker.

## Summary

- On a Friday heading into a long weekend, the session is often too disorganized for hit-and-run trading unless the setup is exceptionally clean.
- If the trader cannot identify a meaningful daily / 4H / 1H pool of liquidity or inefficiency, ICT's answer is to stay flat rather than keep pushing size into a low-quality tape.
- Multiple NDOG / NWOG levels layered near price act like speed bumps or barriers; when price slices through them aggressively it is showing strong intent, and when it chops around them it is telling you the day is still muddy.
- The lecture restates the opening rule for the morning frame: the first valid 1-minute FVG between 9:31 and 10:00 ET is the first-presented FVG for the RTH session.
- ICT shows an immediate rebalance by watching a candle expand, then trade right back to the prior candle's opening price / reference, which is treated as a sharp delivery event rather than a slow FVG revisit.
- The opening price of the relevant bearish candle is explicitly framed as the change in state of delivery, linking the order-block entry back to a precise price marker.
- By the end of the example, ICT is treating the mid-gap / three-quarter-gap checkpoints as live management references while still preferring the cleaner higher-timeframe draw over small intraday noise.

## Practical takeaways

- Use the holiday-calendar context as part of your no-trade filter.
- If the chart is congested with NDOG / NWOG / ORG references and the market is not cutting through them cleanly, reduce aggression or stand aside.
- Keep the first-presented FVG rule strict: 9:31 or later, not the 9:30 candle.
- Treat immediate rebalance as a rapid repricing signature, not as an excuse to scalp every tiny bounce.
- Let the candle opening price / CISD logic do the work when the order-block entry is obvious; do not force the trade before the market has committed.
- Favor the day’s larger liquidity / inefficiency target over short-term churn when the session is thin.
