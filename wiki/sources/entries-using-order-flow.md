# Entries using Order Flow

## Summary

- The clip argues that entries are the easiest part; the hard work happens on the higher time frame where direction, narrative, and context are defined.
- It reduces all entries to fair value gaps, then splits the execution logic into two families: sharp turns and lower-time-frame orderflow lags.
- Sharp turns use an FVG in to an FVG out inside the context area; orderflow entries use stacked lower-time-frame FVGs / lags in the direction of the move.
- Time matters: higher-time-frame news events create the best context, while lower-time-frame sessions / killzones become relevant for the actual trigger.
- The speaker emphasizes volatility windows, New York local time, and instrument-specific trade windows rather than mystical clock times.
- The lesson ends with strict risk management: choose a baseline entry, keep risk small, avoid overtrading, and stop after one trade per day unless data says otherwise.
