# 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 9 - Power Of 3 & New York PM Session Opportunities

**Video**: [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iZLXnNiZm_s) | **Duration**: 27:50 | **Channel**: The Inner Circle Trader | **Published**: February 15, 2022

## Summary

This episode introduces the **Power of 3** — the foundational three-phase pattern of **Accumulation → Manipulation → Distribution** that ICT identifies as the core algorithmic structure behind daily price action. It features a live **NASDAQ E-Mini (NQ)** example from February 14–15, 2022, demonstrating how to identify the Power of 3 on the daily chart, avoid the morning session after overnight runs, and execute in the **New York PM session** (post-lunch, 2:00 PM onward). The episode also covers limit order entries at order blocks + FVG zones, stop placement below short-term lows, and the critical discipline of reducing trade frequency during choppy conditions.

### Core themes

1. **Power of 3** — the three-phase pattern: Accumulation (opening near low) → Manipulation (fake move/Judas swing lower) → Distribution (rally and close near high). This is the bullish version; the bearish version reverses the sequence.
2. **Judas swing** — the false move that occurs in London and NY sessions to trigger stops before the real directional delivery.
3. **Avoid morning session after overnight runs** — if there's a big move overnight for equities, avoid the NY morning session; wait until post-lunch (1:00 PM onward) for cleaner setups.
4. **PM session execution** — the afternoon session (2:00 PM onward) offers better setups after consolidation from overnight moves.
5. **Order block + FVG entry** — consecutive down-closed candles before a surge form the bullish order block; the FVG within that block is the entry zone.
6. **Limit orders at identified levels** — enter at short-term low or minus one tick, not by chasing.
7. **Stop below short-term low / FVG** — allow for normal market noise and imperfection.
8. **Reduce frequency in choppy conditions** — high-frequency trading during consolidation = account blowup risk.

## Key sections

### Power of 3

**Power of 3 = Accumulation → Manipulation → Distribution**

The foundational trading pattern for bullish bias:
1. **Opening price** near the low of the day/session.
2. **Market trades lower** making an important low (fake move / Judas swing).
3. **Rallies and creates a high**.
4. **Closes near the high of the day**.

> "It's not important for you to try to predict the closing price what I'm trying to train you to anticipate is the likelihood of the market making some kind of fake move like a Judas swing"

**Bearish version** (reversed):
1. Opening price near the high.
2. Market trades higher (Judas swing up).
3. Sells off and creates a low.
4. Closes near the low of the day.

### Fair value gaps (FVG)

- Created when price leaves an imbalance between candle bodies.
- Identified by gaps between the high of one candle and low of the next.
- Market typically returns to fill these gaps.
- Use as reference points for entry/exit levels.

### Order blocks

- **Bullish Order Block**: Consecutive down-closed candles before a price surge.
- Mark the area where institutional stops are likely placed.
- High probability entry when price returns to order block after displacement.

> "Consecutive down closed candles right before a price surge that has an imbalance — that's how you find your order blocks"

### Judas swing

- False move that typically occurs in London and New York sessions.
- Market runs one direction to trigger stops, then reverses.
- Critical for identifying optimal entry points.
- The "manipulation" phase of the Power of 3.

### NASDAQ E-Mini futures analysis (February 14–15, 2022)

**February 14 setup**:
- **Daily chart context**: Swing high and swing low established; market traded into deep discount (below 50% equilibrium level); indecisive candle at close (open ≈ close, minimal body); created imbalance from low to high.
- **Key levels**: Fair Value Gap high-end parameter identified; bullish order block formed from down-closed candles; opening price at 8:30 AM used as reference point.
- **Price action**: Traded below opening price ✓; entered imbalance area ✓; hit order block ✓; rallied toward FVG high-end (didn't quite reach it).

**February 15 overnight move**:
- **Critical insight**: Massive overnight rally in London/early morning session.

> "If there's a big move overnight for equities... avoid the New York session don't even mess with the New York session wait until the other side of lunch at 1:00 in the afternoon New York time"

**Why avoid morning after overnight run**:
- Retail traders chase the move at open.
- Creates choppy, sloppy price action.
- High probability of getting "chopped up."
- Better setups emerge in afternoon session.

### Afternoon session strategy (New York PM)

**Optimal timing**:
- **New York Lunch**: Noon to 1:00 PM local time.
- **Best entry window**: 2:00 PM onward (1400 on TradingView).
- Avoid morning session after overnight runs.

**Setup mechanics**:
1. Identify overnight run direction (don't chase it).
2. Wait for consolidation after big move.
3. Look for relative equal lows formed in morning.
4. Anticipate afternoon low that takes out sell-side liquidity.
5. Enter on rally from that low using FVG + order block logic.

**Real example: February 15, 2:34 PM**:
- **Short-term low**: 14,528.5
- **Limit order entry**: 14,520.25 or 14,528 (even number)
- **Stop loss**: Below short-term low (in fair value gap)
- **Risk**: ~14.25 handles

**Risk/reward calculation (6 Micro Contracts)**:
- Risk: $85
- Potential Reward: ~$300
- Ratio: ~3.5:1

**Actual heat on position**:
- 9 points of drawdown = $54 on 6 micros
- Position still profitable despite heat

> "You can't expect it to be perfect... you can't be expecting to be in there at the lowest point no draw down no heat on the position and then it just goes in your favor"

### Critical trading rules

**Entry rules**:
- Use **limit orders** at identified levels (order block + FVG).
- Entry point: Short-term low or minus one tick.
- Don't chase overnight moves.
- Wait for consolidation signals.

**Stop loss rules**:
- Place stop **below the short-term low**.
- Stop should be **below the fair value gap**.
- Allow for volatility/imperfection in price delivery.
- Don't get stopped out by normal market noise.

**Position management**:
- Trust the setups once entered.
- Accept that stops may be tested.
- One losing trade ≠ career failure.
- Look for next setup if stopped out.

**Frequency management**:
- **High frequency trading** in choppy conditions = account blowup risk.
- Reduce trade frequency when:
  - Overnight big moves occur.
  - Consolidation/sideways action dominates.
  - Volatility is extreme.
- Wait for **better setups** with clearer structure.

### Pattern recognition: two-setup scenario

**Setup 1 (Primary)**:
- Swing high broken.
- Fair value gap created.
- Short-term low formed.
- Entry on rally from low.

**Setup 2 (Secondary)**:
- If Setup 1 fails or is missed.
- Look for second FVG / order block.
- Often forms later in session.
- Same logic, different level.

## Extra linkage

- This episode is **the earliest ICT-direct source for the Power of 3** concept in the 2022 mentorship. The three-phase pattern (accumulation → manipulation → distribution) is later formalized as a core ICT model and appears extensively in 2023–2025 material.
- The **Judas swing** is the "manipulation" phase — a false move designed to trigger retail stops before the real directional delivery. This concept is foundational to understanding why price often moves against the obvious direction at session opens.
- The **"avoid morning after overnight run"** rule is critical for the 9:30–11:00 AM window. If NQ has already made a large move overnight (London/early morning), the 9:30 open is likely to be choppy and sloppy. The episode explicitly advises waiting until **post-lunch (1:00 PM onward)** for cleaner setups. This is a key operator discipline for your trading window.
- The **PM session opportunity** (2:00 PM onward) is an alternative trading window when the morning session is invalidated by overnight action. The setup mechanics are the same: relative equal lows → liquidity sweep → MSS → FVG entry.
- The **order block identification rule** — "consecutive down-closed candles right before a price surge that has an imbalance" — is one of the earliest ICT-direct formulations for finding high-probability order blocks. This is later refined but not contradicted.
- The **limit order discipline** — enter at short-term low or minus one tick, don't chase — aligns with the "embrace imperfection" mindset from Episode 7. The trader accepts that the entry won't be perfect and that some heat/drawdown is normal.
- The **risk/reward example** (3.5:1 on 6 micros, $85 risk for ~$300 reward) provides a concrete position sizing reference. The episode shows that even with 9 points of drawdown ($54 heat), the position remains viable if the stop is properly placed below the FVG.
- The **"reduce frequency in choppy conditions"** rule is an anti-overtrading discipline. High-frequency trading during consolidation or after overnight runs increases blowup risk. The operator waits for clearer structure.
