# 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 12 - Advanced Market Structure

**Video**: [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8GkQfdAXZP0) | **Duration**: 1:13:58 | **Channel**: The Inner Circle Trader | **Published**: February 24, 2022

## Summary

ICT's self-described **"advanced price action theory"** lesson — material he says even his paid mentorship group has yet to see. This episode formalizes a **hierarchical market structure framework** with three distinct levels (long-term, intermediate-term, short-term) governed by imbalance and rebalancing. It also defines the **three core questions** every trader must answer before trading, refines the **order block definition** (consecutive series with imbalance, NOT "last candle before reversal"), establishes **candle-direction resistance/support rules**, and positions the **daily chart as paramount** for institutional order flow.

### Core themes

1. **Three core pre-trade questions** — Is price going up for buyside liquidity? Going down for sellside liquidity? Rebalancing an imbalance? Answer these before touching the charts.
2. **Market structure hierarchy** — long-term (daily chart), intermediate-term (created when imbalance rebalances), short-term (individual swing points). Three levels, not two.
3. **Intermediate-term high/low defined by rebalancing** — a swing becomes intermediate-term specifically when an imbalance is rebalanced at that level. Has lower short-term highs on both left and right.
4. **Intermediate-term high violation = red flag** — if price breaks above an intermediate-term high in a bearish thesis, the bias is likely wrong.
5. **Order block refinement** — NOT "the last candle before a reversal"; IS a consecutive series of same-direction candles that contains an imbalance.
6. **Directional candle resistance/support** — up-closed candles act as resistance in bearish moves; down-closed candles act as support in bullish moves.
7. **Daily chart primacy** — institutions work off the daily; all lower-timeframe trades must align with daily structure.
8. **Fibonacci targeting** — specific levels (-1.618, -1.272, -1, 0.618, 0.5, 0.382) for projecting targets from structure points.

## Key sections

### The three core questions

ICT states this is the #1 question before sitting down at the charts:

1. **Is the market likely to go UP for buy-side liquidity / buy stops?**
2. **Is the market likely to go DOWN for sell-side liquidity / sell stops?**
3. **Is the market rebalancing an imbalance (fair value gap)?**

> "That is the number one question that I have before I sit down in front of my charts."

This single framework integrates liquidity targeting and imbalance rebalancing into one pre-trade checklist.

### Market structure hierarchy

ICT moves beyond the retail "higher high / higher low" model into a formal three-level hierarchy:

| Level | Timeframe anchor | Defining characteristic |
|-------|-----------------|------------------------|
| **Long-term high/low** | Daily chart | Framed by higher-timeframe resistance/support; institutional reference |
| **Intermediate-term high/low** | Hourly / 15-minute | Created when an **imbalance rebalances** at that swing point |
| **Short-term high/low** | Lower timeframes | Individual swing points; the most granular level |

**Key principle**: The intermediate-term level is the structural pivot. It is created specifically by **rebalancing** — when price returns to fill an FVG and that retracement swing becomes an intermediate-term high or low.

**Intermediate-term high rules**:
- Formed when an imbalance rebalances at that swing point.
- Must have a **lower short-term high to its left AND right**.
- If it is higher than both adjacent short-term highs, it is a true intermediate-term high.
- **Should NOT be violated** if your directional bias is correct.

> "If the intermediate term high is not higher than two short-term highs, that is telling you that the market is very weak and the algorithm is tipping its hand."

**Violation as red flag**: If price breaks above an intermediate-term high when your bias is bearish, the trade idea is wrong.

### Imbalance and rebalancing as structure creators

**Imbalance**: A single candle (or series) that creates a gap in price — typically a large directional move without balance.

**Rebalancing**: The market returns to fill that imbalance, creating balance in price delivery.

**Critical insight**: When an imbalance rebalances, the **swing high/low at that point becomes an intermediate-term high/low**. This is what elevates a mere swing point to structural significance.

This connects directly to the FVG concept — the FVG is the imbalance, and the retracement into it (rebalancing) creates the intermediate-term level.

### Institutional order flow vs. retail patterns

**Bearish market structure (price going lower)**:
- **Up-closed candles** act as resistance / speed bumps.
- Price should NOT trade above these up-closed candles.
- If price violates an up-closed candle, the bearish idea is likely flawed.
- Down-closed candles support the move lower.

**Bullish market structure (price going higher)**:
- **Down-closed candles** act as support.
- Price should NOT trade below these down-closed candles.
- If price violates a down-closed candle, the bullish idea is likely flawed.
- Up-closed candles support the move higher.

> "When price is moving higher, generally the candles will be predominantly green... but down-closed candles should support price."

### Order block — refined definition

ICT explicitly distinguishes his definition from the common SMC interpretation:

**What an order block IS NOT**: The last candle before a reversal.

**What an order block IS**: A **consecutive series** of candles (all up-closed or all down-closed) that contains an **imbalance** within it. This series acts as institutional support/resistance and can be used for precise entry targeting.

| Type | Composition | Role |
|------|------------|------|
| **Bearish OB** | Consecutive up-closed candles with imbalance inside | Resistance / short entry |
| **Bullish OB** | Consecutive down-closed candles with imbalance inside | Support / long entry |

This definition is consistent with the "consecutive opposite-color candles" framing already in the wiki, but adds the explicit requirement that the series must **contain an imbalance** internally.

### Daily chart primacy

> "The daily chart — that's exactly what institutions are working off of, that's exactly what banks are working off of, that's where the money is."

**Timeframe hierarchy (top-down)**:
1. **Daily chart** — determines bias and long-term direction.
2. **Hourly chart** — frames the trade setup.
3. **15-minute chart** — entry/exit signals ("bellwether").
4. **Lower timeframes** (5-min, 4-min, 1-min) — precision entries.

All lower-timeframe trades must align with daily structure. The daily contains the bulk of institutional volume.

### Fibonacci targeting

ICT specifies the exact Fibonacci levels used for projecting price targets from structure points (anchor from intermediate-term high to long-term low for downside, or intermediate-term low to long-term high for upside):

- **-1.618** (extension)
- **-1.272** (extension)
- **-1** (100% projection)
- **0.618** (61.8% retracement)
- **0.5** (50% retracement)
- **0.382** (38.2% retracement)
- **0** (anchor point)

In the modern ICT framework, Fibonacci is a **targeting tool**, not an entry tool — FVG has taken the entry role.

### Red flags — when your trade idea is wrong

ICT provides concrete error-detection rules:

1. **Intermediate-term high is violated** in a bearish thesis → bias is wrong.
2. **Up-closed candles are violated** in a bearish move → algo is not delivering bearish.
3. **Down-closed candles are violated** in a bullish move → algo is not delivering bullish.
4. **Short-term highs are equal to or above the intermediate-term high** → market is weak, algo is tipping its hand.
