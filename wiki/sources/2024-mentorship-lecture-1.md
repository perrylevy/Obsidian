# ICT 2024 Mentorship — Lecture 1 (August 5, 2024)

Foundational opening lecture of the 2024 public mentorship series. ICT frames the series as teaching his son Caleb from scratch, over live price action on NQ/MNQ futures, Monday–Friday. Lecture 1 is primarily mindset and the first framework for determining bias.

---

## Summary

- **Purpose of the mentorship**: Teaching Caleb — and by extension the public — from scratch, live, with no hindsight. ICT explicitly frames real-time live price action over market replay as the only credible teaching method (@ 11:29–12:23).
- **Primary asset class**: NASDAQ futures (NQ/MNQ). Forex will be revisited when relevant but is not the current focus (@ 9:57–10:18).
- **Time is the first hallmark**: Price delivery is algorithmic and time-based. ICT states that a market move will occur at a specific time or within a 20-minute window ("that's a macro") and that without the correct time element, no setup works regardless of the trader or method (@ 14:09–14:35, @ 1:40:58–1:41:14).
- **Core pre-session framework**: Sit down between two specific windows — 8:00–8:30 NY time or 9:00–9:30 NY time — and identify relative equal highs and relative equal lows on the 15-minute, 5-minute, and 1-minute charts. The smooth side is the draw, and the lecture also distinguishes the broader first-hour range as 9:30–10:30 when bias is still unresolved (@ 57:42–58:06, @ 1:21:10–1:21:47, @ 1:51:44–1:52:32).
- **Bias via relative equal highs/lows**: Whichever side of the market is "smooth" (relative equal highs or lows) is where price will be drawn. The slightly higher high-to-the-left / slightly higher low-to-the-right rule identifies which swing is high-probability for a sweep (@ 1:15:39–1:16:08, @ 1:17:05–1:18:04).
- **Three morning time windows**: 7:00–7:30, 8:00–8:30, and 9:00–9:30 NY time. If the relative equal high/low is not swept in the first window it raises probability for the next window (@ 1:46:00–1:46:23, @ 2:05:17–2:06:07).
- **Journaling discipline**: Screenshot, annotate, record how many candles the move took, record the physical/emotional response. Building baseline data before trading with real money (@ 1:02:14–1:02:58, @ 1:13:26–1:13:50).

## Key Quotes

### Time as the algorithmic foundation

> "It's going to move at a specific time or a period of time within 20 minutes — that's a macro."
> (@ 14:09–14:35)

> "Without the time elements none of it matters."
> (@ 2:14:10–2:14:34)

> "If it's not the right time of day it is absolutely not going to work — period, end of story. Put anybody as the mentor, anybody as the trader… it will fail more times than it [works]."
> (@ 1:40:58–1:41:14)

### New York local time as the reference clock

> "Everything I mentioned in terms of time — if it's the time that matches New York local time… they're never going to not be there."
> (@ 57:42–58:06)

> "The algorithm is on New York local time. I don't care what anybody else tells you. That's what it's on, period."
> (@ 1:20:13–1:20:37)

### The pre-session study framework

> "Between 8:00 and 8:30 in the morning New York local time… you want to sit down and determine where are the smooth locations in price action on the 15-minute, the 5-minute, and the 1-minute."
> (@ 57:42–58:06, @ 1:21:10–1:21:27)

> "15-minute, 5-minute, and 1-minute — those are your first three time frames to worry about."
> (@ 56:45–57:16)

### Morning session windows and the pre-market / opening range distinction

> "7:00 to 7:30 — that is your pre-market range. 7:30 to 8:00 is your opening range… 9 to 9:30 that's your pre-market range, and then post 9:30 to 10 o'clock that's your opening range. There is no 15-minute opening range — it's a 30-minute opening range. That's algorithmic."
> (@ 1:51:44–1:52:32)

> "The framework begins at 7 o'clock in the morning. Start looking for relative equal highs to form — not before 7 o'clock."
> (@ 2:05:45–2:06:07)

> "You have three opportunities… as each one of these time windows passes by… it increases the likelihood of it going there."
> (@ 2:05:17–2:05:45)

### Morning session start time

> "The morning session — am session — starts at 7 o'clock in the morning, New York time. It matters not that we're trading Forex or Futures."
> (@ 1:42:45–1:43:01)

### Relative equal highs / lows — high-probability rule

> "If you have two swing highs and the one to the left is slightly higher than the right, you have a very very high probability that they're going to want to take it above there."
> (@ 1:15:39–1:16:08)

> "A high probability relative equal low that the market is likely to draw down and go below — it would be a low that has a slightly higher low."
> (@ 1:17:22–1:18:04)

### Draw on liquidity defined

> "Draw on liquidity is just an initial reasonable assumption on our part to determine a directional move that could unfold. It does not define the absolute terminus to a price run."
> (@ 1:06:38–1:07:05)

### What the algorithm reaches for

> "It's reaching for old highs, old lows, and inefficiencies — that's the fair value gap."
> (@ 58:39–59:12)

### Order block post-8:30

> "That down-closed candle is an order block. We have displacement. It took liquidity. And the market has been one-directional. What makes that order block specifically is it has happened post 8:30. Now it could have formed right at 8:30 because 8:30 the algorithm will start spooling for liquidity or inefficiencies."
> (@ 53:24–54:06)

### SIBI / BISI candle science

> "A fair value gap that has a down close is a CISD [sell-side imbalance, buyside inefficiency — SIBI]. A fair value gap that has an up close is a BISI — buyside imbalance, sellside inefficiency."
> (@ 1:01:59–1:02:14)

> [!note] Uncited
> The exact acronym expansions ICT uses for SIBI/BISI are stated quickly and partially garbled in the transcript at 1:01:59. The glosses above match prevailing wiki usage but should be verified against a cleaner source.

### Inversion FVG usage

> "We can see how that [FVG] becomes an inversion fair value gap here and then it sends price higher, retraces down, and then you have your second leg redistribution."
> (@ 2:04:30–2:04:57)

### Event Horizon

> "Event Horizon — which is the midpoint between two new week opening gaps or new day opening gaps."
> (@ 1:07:53–1:08:19)

### Macro (20-minute window)

> "It's going to move at a specific time or a period of time within 20 minutes — that's a macro."
> (@ 14:09–14:35)

### One trade per day / one micro contract — beginner rule

> "If you trade with one micro contract and you only take one trade a day, it's physically impossible for you to go out on day one and blow the account."
> (@ 1:03:16–1:03:42)

### London session acknowledgment

> "Your place in the world… may not be conducive for you to trade at 8:30 in the morning New York local time. You may have to trade in the London session — and yes, I will be up during the London session doing one of these lectures in a week or two."
> (@ 1:12:54–1:13:26)
