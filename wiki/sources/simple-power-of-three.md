# This Simple Power of 3 Strategy Changed My Trading

## Summary

- Power of three is presented as the foundation of the presenter's trading: accumulation, manipulation, distribution — the three phases that form every candle at every timeframe (@ 0:01, @ 0:15).
- Bearish variant: accumulation at the open, manipulation above the open, then distribution lower to close. Bullish is the mirror: accumulation at the open, manipulation lower, run higher to close (@ 0:15).
- Context must be set from the economic calendar; for index traders the dollar index news matters. FOMC weeks in particular make Monday and Tuesday choppy because professionals wait for the event before positioning (@ 1:37, @ 2:06, @ 2:22).
- Post-FOMC behavior: if higher-timeframe liquidity targets aren't hit, the market tends to continue through Thursday/Friday; if a higher-timeframe level is hit Thursday/Friday, price can reverse and take out the FOMC candle (@ 2:30, @ 2:51).
- The weekly playbook for the example week: trade lower Mon/Tue into FOMC, then reverse higher to take out the previous week's high — a weekly power of three (@ 2:51, @ 8:23).
- Long accumulation below the weekly open: extended price action below the weekly open is interpreted as accumulation of long positions before the expansion higher (@ 7:05).
- Trending Asian range rule: when the Asian range is trending, 50% of it is typically respected and the low often isn't raided until another session (@ 7:31).
- FOMC rate release first move (2 p.m.) is typically the false move / intraday high or low; the real move comes once the Fed starts speaking (@ 5:57).
- This source is a framing example: it is valuable because it restates the same doctrine in a compact, repeatable way, even though the concepts themselves are already supported elsewhere in the wiki.
