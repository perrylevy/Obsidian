## Summary

Episode 16 is a broad execution lesson on how to frame the day from the higher timeframe, then trade multiple intraday setups as the session unfolds. It reinforces that the daily chart is the primary map, that liquidity pools and premium/discount context matter more than retail logic, and that both the midnight and 8:30 opens can be used as opening-price references.

### Core themes

1. **Daily chart first** — the majority of analysis belongs on the daily chart.
2. **Multiple setups per session** — one move does not exhaust the day; retracements can create new opportunities.
3. **Relative equal highs/lows** — nearby highs/lows advertise liquidity pools.
4. **Premium / discount framework** — the 50% level defines whether price is expensive or cheap.
5. **Opening-price references** — midnight NY and 8:30 AM NY both matter as anchors.
6. **FVG / OB structure** — imbalances and order blocks are the preferred decision areas, not retail supply/demand heuristics.
7. **Stacked FVG logic** — when gaps are stacked, anticipate price reaching the upper gap first.
8. **Fibonacci swing projection** — use candle bodies, not wicks, to project targets.
9. **Multiple-contract management** — scale out at the first target, let a runner continue, and move stop to breakeven.
10. **Lunch-hour restraint** — avoid new entries during the 12:00–1:00 PM window.

## Key sections

### Higher-timeframe framing

ICT says the daily chart is the main map: the question is where price is likely to expand, higher or lower. The session setup is then traded against that higher-timeframe expectation rather than against retail noise.

### Session structure

The summary defines the morning session as roughly **8:30 AM to noon NY time**, with the lunch hour as a typical avoid-entry window. The lesson is not that there is only one setup; rather, multiple setups can emerge as the market reprices through the session.

### Liquidity and reference levels

Relative equal highs and lows are described as liquidity pools. The algorithm is said to target where the easy money is, not where retail believes the market should go.

The episode also notes both the **midnight NY open** and the **8:30 AM NY open** as important reference points for manipulation and bias confirmation.

### Premium / discount and arrays

The 50% midpoint is the dividing line between expensive and cheap. ICT frames bullish order blocks as down-close candle clusters acting as support in discount, and uses FVGs and order blocks as the practical decision zones.

### Fibonacci and target projection

The summary emphasizes that Fibonacci should be anchored to candle bodies, not wicks, and used to project a measured move / target distance. The example shows a daily order block and a Fibonacci projection aligning nearly perfectly with the actual low.

### Management

The multiple-contract framework is simple: take one contract off at the first objective, let the remainder run, and move the stop to breakeven after the first target is met.
