# ICT 2024 Mentorship — Lecture #12 (August 19, 2024)

Lecture 12 extends the August mentorship from morning execution into full-day trade management. ICT uses a bullish daily / weekly bias, a 9:30-or-lower low-risk buy, the lunch-macro retracement, and the 3:15 / 3:45 last-hour routines to show how a trader can hold an intraday entry for a much larger daily-range delivery instead of treating every opening move as a scalping-only event.

## Summary

- The lecture starts from higher-timeframe bullish orderflow: daily and weekly targets remain overhead, so intraday shorts are treated as smaller countertrend opportunities while longs are the heavier-handed side.
- ICT reframes the **Power of Three** as the answer to the classic problem of buying below the open while still bullish. The 9:30 opening price and any move below it can be treated as Judas-swing / false-move territory inside a bullish daily candle.
- The live entry is presented as an **institutional orderflow entry drill** / low-risk buy: price trades below the morning opening reference, interacts with NWOG / order-block support, and then reclaims higher delivery.
- The key holding lesson is time-based: if the daily candle is expected to close near its high, the trader has to submit to the time required for that candle to form instead of strangling the trade too early.
- Lecture 12 makes the **lunch macro** more explicit: at 11:30, look backward for the first low formed after 10:00; that low is the obvious liquidity target the macro is likely to raid before the trend resumes.
- The afternoon sequence is split into two pieces: the **3:15–3:45 last-hour macro** can create a pullback off the high, then **3:45–4:00 market-on-close** can disregard that pullback and spool back toward the higher-timeframe target.
- The source also reinforces that once the larger daily objective has substantially delivered, new longs can still be valid but should usually be traded with lighter size because the bulk of the daily move has already happened.

## Practical takeaways

- When daily / weekly bias is strongly bullish, size long setups more confidently than countertrend shorts.
- A 9:30 opening-price dip can be a buy, not a reason to panic, if it matches the higher-timeframe bullish framework.
- If a morning long is working, do not automatically tighten the stop to death before lunch; the lunch macro often raids the first low formed after 10:00 before continuation.
- If the thesis is a bullish daily candle, use 3:15 and then 3:45 as hold / reassessment checkpoints rather than exiting just because the trade is already green.
- Once the higher-timeframe objective has largely been met, reduce aggression and stop forcing heavy size.
