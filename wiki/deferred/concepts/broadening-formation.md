# Broadening Formation

A broadening formation is an expanding range in which successive highs and lows extend beyond the prior boundaries. On the higher timeframe, it behaves like an outside bar that has taken both sides of the range ([[sources/broadening-formations-ict-seek-destroy]] @ 0:36, @ 9:01).

## How to spot it

- Identify a local high and low.
- Extend the lines through the swing highs and swing lows that have already been taken.
- When price keeps failing one side and then trading back through the range, the formation broadens rather than compresses ([[sources/broadening-formations-ict-seek-destroy]] @ 1:21, @ 1:49, @ 2:14, @ 2:41).

## Practical use

- On consolidations, the edges of the broadening formation become points of interest for continuation or reversal.
- The pattern can be used as a target map: if price fails one side of the range, the opposite side often becomes the next objective.
- TTrades pairs it with [[concepts/smt|SMT]] on the edges, and sometimes uses [[models/power-of-three|Power of Three]] / daily profile logic as an additional framing layer for expansion-reversal sequencing ([[sources/broadening-formations-ict-seek-destroy]] @ 5:25, @ 6:48, @ 7:07, @ 9:56).

## Source framing

The Seek & Destroy clip is a practical example of the same range-expansion logic: connect the highs and lows that have already been swept, then project the next edge as the likely target. Treat it as a framing example for the broader broadening-formation concept, not as a separate doctrine family.

## Higher-timeframe note

A broadening formation on the higher timeframe often shows up as an outside bar that has taken both the prior range high and prior range low ([[sources/broadening-formations-ict-seek-destroy]] @ 8:39, @ 9:01).
