# External Liquidity

External liquidity is the liquidity resting at the **obvious swing highs and swing lows** of a range. It is the visible stop pool that breakout traders and trend followers tend to cluster around ([[concepts/liquidity]], [[sources/internal-external-liquidity]] @ 0:58, @ 1:17).

## Core idea

External liquidity is the classic ERL side of the liquidity map:
- highs become buyside liquidity,
- lows become sellside liquidity,
- and those points are often the first obvious targets for raid, sweep, or continuation ([[concepts/buyside-liquidity]], [[concepts/sellside-liquidity]], [[concepts/liquidity-sweep]]).

## How it behaves

The internal-external model treats external liquidity as the market’s visible boundary markers:
- price can sweep them and reverse,
- or run through them and continue,
- but either way they are the obvious stops the algorithm can consume first ([[concepts/liquidity]], [[models/internal-external-liquidity-model]], [[sources/internal-external-liquidity]] @ 1:28).

## Why it matters

External liquidity matters because it defines the range edges and often sets the next draw on liquidity. Prior day highs/lows, prior session highs/lows, and relative equal highs/lows all fall into this family of targets ([[concepts/relative-equal-highs-lows]], [[concepts/draw-on-liquidity]], [[sources/internal-external-liquidity]] @ 11:16, [[sources/2023-mentorship-silver-bullet]] @ 3:47–6:41).
