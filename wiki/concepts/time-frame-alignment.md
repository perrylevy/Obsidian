# Time-Frame Alignment

Time-frame alignment is the rule that lower-timeframe entries should be taken inside a higher-timeframe context area, not as isolated patterns. In the IFVG source, the failure mode is explicitly trading the pattern without HTF swing-point context ([[sources/how-to-trade-ifvg]] @ 1:07, [[sources/entries-using-order-flow]] @ 1:34).

## Core idea

The higher timeframe sets the narrative; the lower timeframe only executes it.

That means an FVG, IFVG, or order-flow trigger is strongest when it is nested inside a larger HTF draw, context area, or swing framework.

A useful working stack is:

- weekly → 4H
- daily → 1H
- 4H → 15m
- 1H → 5m
- 30m → 3m
- 15m → 1m

The point of the stack is not the pair itself; it is making sure bias, structure, and entry are aligned before execution.

The 03/21/2026 market commentary gives a direct macro example: when the dollar index is trapped inside a wide consolidation, the proper move is to step up the timeframe and stop trying to force a lower-timeframe read without higher-timeframe alignment ([[sources/ict-2026-market-commentary-march-21-2026]]).

The 02/13/2025 Telegram execution is a nested-stack live example: the higher-time-frame buy-side draw, the opening-range-gap frame, and the first-presented FVG all had to line up before the long could be managed, rather than treated as separate ideas ([[sources/nq-live-execution-using-analysis-i-shared-in-telegram-02132025]] @ 3:17, @ 6:12, @ 9:03, @ 10:06, @ 14:43, @ 15:30).

The "Keys To Success In Troubled Markets" lecture is another reminder to step up the timeframe when the market is distorted and the lower-timeframe tape is not resolving cleanly ([[sources/2025-lecture-series-keys-to-success-in-troubled-markets-june-16-2025]] @ 10:23, @ 10:41).

The "This Stupid Simple Strategy Works Everyday" clip is a clean 1H/4H → 5m example of the same nesting rule: the higher-time-frame FVG sets the context, the 5-minute FVG supplies the trigger, and the inversion only matters because it is nested inside the larger draw. Re-entry after a stop-out is only sensible when that higher-time-frame draw is still intact ([[sources/this-stupid-simple-strategy-works-everyday-stupid-simple-and-proven]]).

## Why it matters

Without alignment:

- the setup can be a random pattern,
- the swing context may be wrong,
- and the entry becomes less reliable.

With alignment:

- the lower-timeframe trigger becomes an execution detail,
- the higher-timeframe bias stays intact,
- and the trade sits inside the correct delivery structure.

A useful session-specific example is the Beesting-at-open model: the higher-time-frame bias must still match the post-open SMT + CISD trigger, otherwise the pattern is just noise around the opening print ([[synthesis/how-to-trade-the-beesting-at-open-model]], [[sources/this-ict-model-will-change-your-trading-forever-smt-cisd-time]], [[sources/this-ict-model-will-make-you-profitable-forever-stop-paying-for-mentorships-beesting-open-vid-2]]).

## Practical use

Before entering, ask:

- What is the HTF bias?
- What is the context area?
- Is this LTF setup just a pattern, or is it aligned with the larger swing?
- Has the structure timeframe already confirmed with a change in the state of delivery or market structure shift?
- Is the entry timeframe only a trigger, or is it actually aligned with the larger swing?
