# No Trade

A **no-trade** is a deliberate decision to stay flat when the tape does not yet offer a clean, structurally legible edge. It is not indecision; it is the correct action when the first hour has not resolved, when high-impact news is about to hit, or when the move has already become one-sided and unattractive.

## Core idea

The no-trade state protects the trader from forcing a thesis before the market has shown its hand. It usually appears when:

- the open is unclear,
- the expected news release has not passed,
- the session is too noisy or one-sided,
- or the first hour has not produced a trustworthy working range.

It keeps the trader from converting uncertainty into random exposure.

The patient-trading clip adds a pre-entry version of the same restraint: boredom and urgency can be just as dangerous as news or an unclear open, because they push the trader to act before the market has offered a real edge.

The "3-Step A+ ICT Strategy" clip sharpens the rule further: if the target is not obvious, or if the market is sitting in a 50/50 balance where external liquidity is not clearly favored, standing aside is better than forcing an internal-liquidity guess.

## Practical use

Use no-trade when:

- there is high-impact news still ahead,
- the market has not established a clean first-hour frame,
- or the setup only exists because you want it to exist.

Lecture 6 of the 2024 mentorship adds a sharper pre-news version of this rule: if Monday morning sits directly in front of major Tuesday / Wednesday releases such as PPI and CPI, treat the session as a one-chance / low-aggression environment. Take the early clean setup only if it is obvious, pay yourself quickly, and do not keep re-entering just because the market later does what you first expected ([[sources/2024-mentorship-lecture-6]]).
The 10/17/2024 news-release tape and 10/04/2024 NFP lecture make the same release-day rule explicit: if the event has not printed yet, or if the first impulse has not resolved the structure, staying flat is the edge-preserving choice ([[sources/ict-2024-mentorship-news-release-tape-reading-october-17-2024]], [[sources/ict-2024-mentorship-october-nfp-nq-october-04-2024]]).
Lecture 21 extends that restraint to holiday Fridays: if the tape is muddy, congested with layered opening gaps, and going into a long weekend, the correct move is often to stand aside or keep size tiny unless a truly clean opportunity appears ([[sources/2024-mentorship-lecture-21]]).

The 10/03/2024 NQ market review is another clean no-trade example: ICT calls NFP a highly manipulated day, says the new trader should sit out most of the week, and shows how the opening-range gap / weekly imbalance can remain unresolved until later in the session ([[sources/october-03-2024-nq-market-review]]).

The June 11, 2025 EURUSD / NQ update extends the same restraint to a CPI / PPI volatility week: if the tape is unstable and the expected IFVG continuation fails, ICT says the market may simply consolidate and the trader should move to the sidelines instead of pressing the edge ([[sources/2025-lecture-series-eurusd-nq-futures-june-11-2025]] @ 3:40, @ 16:49, @ 18:07).

When in doubt, stand aside and let the market finish the opening work.
