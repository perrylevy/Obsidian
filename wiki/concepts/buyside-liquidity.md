# Buyside Liquidity

Buyside liquidity is the pool of resting **buy-stops above swing highs**. In practical ICT usage, it is the visible high-side liquidity that breakout traders leave behind when they place stops above prior highs ([[concepts/liquidity]], [[sources/internal-external-liquidity]] @ 0:58, @ 1:17, [[sources/how-to-trade-turtle-soups]] @ 1:39, @ 3:17).

## Core idea

When price reaches a buyside pool, it can either:
- **sweep** it and reverse, or
- **run** through it and continue higher.

That sweep-vs-run distinction is central to turtle soup and liquidity-sweep logic ([[concepts/liquidity-sweep]], [[models/turtle-soup]], [[sources/how-to-trade-turtle-soups]] @ 3:48, @ 4:15, @ 8:30).

## Why it matters

Buyside liquidity is not just a chart label. It is one of the market’s most common targets for delivery:
- it marks where breakout buy orders cluster,
- it often becomes the draw on liquidity above price,
- and it can serve as the trap level that fuels a reversal after a sweep ([[concepts/draw-on-liquidity]], [[sources/internal-external-liquidity]] @ 1:28, [[sources/2023-mentorship-silver-bullet]] @ 3:47–6:41).

## Common forms

Buyside liquidity often appears as:
- prior swing highs,
- relative equal highs,
- prior session highs,
- prior day highs,
- or other obvious highs that attract stop placement ([[concepts/relative-equal-highs-lows]], [[concepts/liquidity]], [[sources/internal-external-liquidity]] @ 11:16, [[sources/2023-mentorship-silver-bullet]] @ 3:47–6:41).

## Sweep behavior

When buyside liquidity is swept:
- price trades above the obvious high,
- breakout traders get trapped,
- and a reversal can follow if the move fails to hold above the level.

That is the classic Turtle Soup / liquidity sweep pattern on the buy side ([[models/turtle-soup]], [[concepts/liquidity-sweep]], [[sources/how-to-trade-turtle-soups]] @ 2:11, @ 2:35, @ 8:30).
