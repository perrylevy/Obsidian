# Daily Bias

**Daily bias** is the directional thesis derived from analyzing the daily chart to determine whether price is likely to draw to an **old high** (bullish bias) or an **old low** (bearish bias). It is the first analytical step before any intraday execution.

## Definition

Daily bias answers: *"Is price likely to draw higher to an old high, or lower to an old low?"*

ICT's **three core pre-trade questions** (from Episode 12) operationalize bias into an actionable checklist:
1. Is the market likely to go UP for buy-side liquidity / buy stops?
2. Is the market likely to go DOWN for sell-side liquidity / sell stops?
3. Is the market rebalancing an imbalance (fair value gap)?

> "That is the number one question that I have before I sit down in front of my charts" ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-12]]).

**Key principle**: Maintain bias until price reaches the target level (old high or old low).

## How to establish daily bias

1. **Identify the range** — find the high and low of the current price action.
2. **Look for market structure shifts** — determine if price has broken above or below key levels.
3. **Count directional candles** — observe the number of down-close candles (bearish bias) or up-close candles (bullish bias).
4. **Expect retracement** — once liquidity is taken out, price typically retraces back into the range.

## Working with daily bias

**With bias (aligned with daily direction)**:
- Use maximum leverage: 3.5–4.5% risk per trade.
- Trade confidently in the biased direction.
- The intraday MSS + FVG sequence confirms the bias.

**Against bias (counter-trend)**:
- Dial back leverage significantly: 0.5–1% risk per trade.
- Reduce exposure to adverse moves.
- Only take high-probability setups against the bias.

## Consolidation hurdles

When price consolidates around **equilibrium** (the 50% midpoint between high and low):
- Bias becomes unclear, even for experienced traders.
- Traditional daily bias signals weaken.
- **Solution**: Shift to intraday charts and hunt liquidity pools with "surgical strikes."
- Don't overstay your welcome in the market.

## Key sources

The 2022 mentorship Episode 7 provides the **earliest ICT-direct daily bias framework** in the mentorship: "Will price draw to old high or old low?" — maintain bias until target is reached ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-7]]).

Episode 10 refines the daily bias determination process with the **opening range** concept: on bearish days, premium arrays form between the open and the high, and the range from open to high can be subtracted from the open to find the optimal short entry zone ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-10]]).

Episode 11 adds the **swing high/low nesting effect** (from Larry Williams): swing highs and lows are classified as single-halo (short-term), double-halo (intermediate), or triple-halo (long-term). The layered nesting reveals directional bias and the hierarchy of structural levels — a triple-halo high carries more weight than a single-halo high ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-11]]).

Episode 12 formalizes the **market structure hierarchy** further with ICT's own framework: **long-term highs/lows** (daily chart), **intermediate-term highs/lows** (created when an imbalance rebalances at that swing point), and **short-term highs/lows** (individual swing points). The intermediate-term level is the structural pivot — it must have lower short-term highs on both sides, and its **violation** is a red flag that the directional bias is wrong ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-12]]). Episode 12 also provides the **three core pre-trade questions** (buyside liquidity? sellside liquidity? rebalancing?), the **candle-direction resistance/support rules** (up-closed = resistance in bearish; down-closed = support in bullish), and positions the **daily chart as paramount** — institutions and banks work off the daily, and all lower-timeframe trades must align with daily structure ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-12]]).

Episode 15 shows the daily bias in live practice: bullish bias at the equity open, swing high breakout as structure confirmation, and the FVG at the 680 level as the entry array within that bullish thesis ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-15]]).

Episode 16 broadens that into a session model: the daily chart remains the primary map, but one session can produce multiple setups as retracements and repricings unfold ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-16]]).
Episode 18 reinforces the daily-bias workflow from the top down: start on the daily chart, mark the prior day's highs and lows as liquidity, then only transpose the thesis lower after the bias is clear ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-18]]).

Episode 22 reinforces the bearish daily-bias read with a daily bearish OB, relative equal lows as the draw, and SMT confirmation that ES is the weaker leg ([[sources/2022-ict-mentorship-episode-22]]).

The 04/29/2024 bias lecture uses the Power of 3 as one of the surrounding delivery frames: seasonal tendencies and long-term macro directional bias drill down to the intraday manipulation window, with accumulation / manipulation / distribution as the repeating structure on every timeframe ([[sources/how-to-read-price-with-or-without-a-bias-april-29-2024]] @ 20:40–21:11, @ 47:18).
