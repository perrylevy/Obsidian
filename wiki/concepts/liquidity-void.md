# Liquidity Void

A liquidity void is a price zone with little or no actual trading between two defined levels. In the opening-gap material, it is the empty space between the prior regular-trading-hours settlement and the new 9:30 a.m. open when the market leaves a large opening range gap. The April 4 NQ NFP review sharpens the distinction: if there are actual prints in the gap, ICT treats that as an inefficiency rather than a true void ([[sources/nq-nfp-algorithmic-price-delivery-04042025]] @ 5:36, @ 5:56).

## Core idea

If price never traded in a zone during the relevant session, that zone can behave like an inefficient pocket that later wants to rebalance.

The 11/14/2025 general market commentary uses the same idea as a risk filter: with large overnight gap risk and a potentially empty price zone, ICT says short-term trading should be reduced rather than forced ([[sources/ict-general-market-commentary-november-14-2025]] @ 11:59, @ 12:23, @ 13:26).

The "Keys To Success In Troubled Markets" lecture uses the same concept when it points to a daily fair value gap / imbalance pocket and warns that the chart can be full of empty space that should not be treated like normal price action ([[sources/2025-lecture-series-keys-to-success-in-troubled-markets-june-16-2025]] @ 2:10, @ 6:31, @ 12:28).

In the opening-range-gap lectures, the void is not treated as a decorative chart artifact. It is a live reference for:

- gap continuation,
- partial rebalancing,
- and later consequent encroachment / midpoint reactions.

## How it is used

The term shows up when the market opens far away from prior settlement and leaves a large gap that may not fully close right away.
